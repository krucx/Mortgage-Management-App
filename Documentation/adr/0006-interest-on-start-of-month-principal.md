# Each month's interest is charged on the Principal at the start of that month

A month's interest is charged on the Principal as it stood at the previous Accrual Date, so paying Principal mid-month lowers interest only from the next month. We chose this over charging on the Principal at the Accrual Date itself (which let a Customer avoid almost a whole month's interest by paying Principal a day before the Accrual Date) and over day-weighted interest (fairest, but every interest line becomes a multi-part calculation). It matches "a month started is a month charged", the same thinking as the Full-month Part-Month Rule. A month starts at the start of its Accrual Date, before that day's Payments: a Payment made that day comes after that day's interest (so paying it still avoids capitalisation) but lowers interest only from the next month, like a Payment on any other day of the month. (Until ADR 0011 a Payment on an Accrual Date counted for the month starting then; with the Interest to Date owed from that day, paying off the Principal then would have let off the whole month.)
