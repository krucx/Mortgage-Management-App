# An Advance is cash received today, applied on later Accrual Dates

A Customer may pay a Mortgage's interest for its next few months at once. Applied as an ordinary Allocation, everything beyond the Interest to Date would pay Principal that day, which the Lender does not want: the point of an Advance is to pay interest as it falls due. So the Advance is part of an ordinary Payment received that day (one Receipt, one Cash Limit check, counted as received that day), and each Mortgage's Advance in it is split into Advance Instalments, each an Allocation dated one of that Mortgage's next Accrual Dates after the date received. Under a Grace Period the Payment keeps both its earlier date and the date received, as any Grace Payment does: its ordinary Allocations take the earlier date, but its Advance Instalments are scheduled from the date received, never the earlier one, so Grace never moves or lets off an Advance. The split is as equal as the paise allow: every part is the Advance divided by their number, rounded down to the paisa, and the last part also takes the remainder, so the parts always add up to the Advance. Payment dates still cannot be in the future; only these Allocations can. Replay (ADR 0001) takes an Advance Instalment only once it reaches its date, where it is applied like any Payment that day: unpaid interest (oldest first), then the Interest to Date, then Principal.

One rule keeps the Lender from ever owing the Customer: what a Mortgage's upcoming Advance Instalments hold may never exceed its Outstanding. An Advance is refused if it would hold more than the Mortgage owes on the date received, after that Payment's other Allocations and Discounts. Interest only adds to the Outstanding and each Instalment lowers both sides equally, so only a later Payment or Discount can break the rule, and its Preview refuses it, naming the upcoming parts to cancel first or the amount that still fits. Within the rule, an Instalment that would reduce Principal is a warning the Lender may accept, at saving or when a later Payment or Term Change makes an Instalment exceed its month's interest; Replay sends that excess to Principal.

The Lender may cancel an upcoming Instalment, which applies its amount at once as an ordinary Allocation on the cancel date. A Closure cancels them all in the same way, before any Final Payment; by the rule above they never pay more than the Outstanding, so a Redemption may need less cash or none, and a Reopen restores them. For the same reason a Mortgage being Redeemed or Waived cannot also take an Advance: being closed, it has no interest to come. New Payment refuses an Advance on a Mortgage it pays in full, and a Waiver takes none. Voiding the Payment removes every Instalment, applied or not.

We considered a pseudo-payment that would exist only from its date, but the cash is in the Lender's hand today and would go unrecorded and unreceipted. We considered holding the money and capping each Instalment at its month's interest, carrying the rest forward, but that brings a running held balance into every Replay. Refunds were left out: the app has never recorded money going back to a Customer, and the rule above means a Closure never needs one. Checking only that the Advance fits by its last Instalment was rejected, because a Closure before then could hold more than the Mortgage owes.
