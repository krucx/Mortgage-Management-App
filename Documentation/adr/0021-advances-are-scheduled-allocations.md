# An Advance is cash received today, applied on later Accrual Dates

A Customer may pay a Mortgage's interest for its next few months at once. Applied as an ordinary Allocation, everything beyond the Interest to Date would pay Principal that day, which the Lender does not want: the point of an Advance is to pay interest as it falls due. So the Payment is recorded on the date received (one Receipt, one Cash Limit check, counted as received that day), and each Mortgage's Advance in it is split equally into Advance Instalments, each an Allocation dated one of that Mortgage's next Accrual Dates after the date received. Payment dates still cannot be in the future; only these Allocations can. Replay (ADR 0001) takes an Advance Instalment only once it reaches its date, where it is applied like any Payment that day: unpaid interest (oldest first), then the Interest to Date, then Principal.

The Advance is checked when saved against a Projection of the Mortgage with every Instalment in place: an Instalment that would reduce Principal is a warning the Lender may accept, but an Advance that would pay more than the Mortgage would owe is refused, so the Lender never ends up owing the Customer. Later changes (another Payment, a Term Change) may make an Instalment exceed its month's interest; their Preview warns, and Replay sends the excess to Principal. The Lender may cancel an upcoming Instalment, which applies its amount at once as an ordinary Allocation on the cancel date; a Closure cancels them all in the same way, and a Reopen restores them. Voiding the Payment removes every Instalment, applied or not.

We considered a pseudo-payment that would exist only from its date, but the cash is in the Lender's hand today and would go unrecorded and unreceipted. We considered holding the money and capping each Instalment at its month's interest, carrying the rest forward, but that brings a running held balance into every Replay. Refunds on cancel were left out: the app has never recorded money going back to a Customer.
