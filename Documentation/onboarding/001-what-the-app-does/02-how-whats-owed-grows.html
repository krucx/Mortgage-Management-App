Chapter 001 · Lesson 2

How what's owed grows

On 19 Oct 2026 Meera asks: "What do I owe you today?" The answer isn't just ₹80,000. Some interest is unpaid, and part of a month has gone by. This lesson shows how the app turns Terms into that one figure.

By the end you can work out a month's interest from a Mortgage's Terms, and say what Outstanding means on any day.

The problem

Interest sounds simple: 2% a month. But a Lender and a Customer have to agree on details that add up to real money.

  • When in the month is the interest added?
  • On what amount, if the Customer paid some back halfway through the month?
  • What if interest goes unpaid for a year? Does the unpaid interest start earning interest too?
  • If Meera comes in on the 19th to pay everything off, what does she owe for the nine days since the 10th?

The three parts of a Mortgage's Terms answer these questions. Together they are the rate, the Interest Mode and the Part-Month Rule.

The idea

Once a month, on the Mortgage's Accrual Date, a month's interest is added: the rate times the Principal as it stood when that month began. Between Accrual Dates, the interest for the month under way builds up day by day as the Interest to Date. What the Mortgage owes on any day, its Outstanding, is the Principal plus all unpaid interest plus that Interest to Date.

The flow

Notice the loop: nothing here waits for a Payment. Interest is added whether or not the Customer pays. Payments, in lesson 3, only pay it off.

The parts, one at a time

The Accrual Date

An Accrual Date is the monthly day on which a month's interest is added. It comes from the start date: Meera's chain started on 10 Nov 2025, so interest is added on the 10th of every month. "Accrue" just means "build up"; interest accrues on the Accrual Date.

The rate

A percentage per month. Meera's Terms say 2%, so each 10th adds 2% of ₹80,000 = ₹1,600.

The Interest Mode and Capitalisation

Interest that is added and not paid sits as unpaid interest. The Interest Mode decides whether, and when, unpaid interest starts earning interest itself. Moving unpaid interest into the Principal is called Capitalisation. Once capitalised, it is Principal, and next month's interest is charged on it.

Interest ModeUnpaid interest is capitalised…
Simplenever; interest never earns interest
Compounded Yearlyevery twelve months, on the start date's anniversary
Compounded Monthlyevery month, on each Accrual Date

The Part-Month Rule and the Interest to Date

The month's interest is only added on the Accrual Date. But a Customer who comes in on the 19th to settle has had the money for nine more days. The Interest to Date is the interest for the month under way, counted from that month's first day through today, with today included. The Part-Month Rule says how to count it:

Part-Month RuleInterest to Date after d days
pro-ratad/30 of the month's interest (every month counts as 30 days)
full monththe whole month's interest, from the month's first day
nothingnothing, until the Accrual Date adds the month

The Interest to Date is never capitalised. On the next Accrual Date it is replaced by the real month's interest.

Outstanding

Outstanding is what a Mortgage owes on a date: Principal + unpaid interest + Interest to Date. It is exactly the sum that would settle the Mortgage and send the jewellery home that day. There is no second "amount to settle" figure. ADR 0011 made it so, because Customers used to be told one figure and then asked to pay a different one.

From May to August she paid ₹1,600 each month, but each Payment first cleared the oldest unpaid month, so one month's worth (₹1,600) stayed unpaid all summer. On 10 Sep, ₹3,200 was unpaid and she paid ₹2,000, leaving ₹1,200. On 10 Oct another ₹1,600 was added: ₹2,800.

On 19 Oct, the month that began on 10 Oct has run 10 days, counting the 10th and the 19th. Pro-rata, that's 10/30 of ₹1,600, which is ₹533. So:

Outstanding = ₹80,000 + ₹2,800 + ₹533 = ₹83,333

Check it: The Gold Chain Story, chapter 4 ("The life of a pledge") shows ₹83,333 on the chain's own page. In chapter 002 you'll serve the sample book and see it yourself.

No Capitalisation happened. Meera's chain is Compounded Yearly, and its first anniversary is 10 Nov 2026. If she pays nothing before then, that day adds November's ₹1,600 and then moves all ₹4,400 of unpaid interest into the Principal, which becomes ₹84,400. From then on 2% is charged on ₹84,400.

Nuances

Compounded Monthly: Arjun's ruby ring

Arjun pledged a ruby ring on 15 Jun 2025 for ₹60,000, on his own Terms: 2.5% a month, Compounded Monthly, pro-rata.

Predict: on 15 Jul 2025 the first month's interest is added and not paid. What is the Principal afterwards, and what will the next month's interest be?

DateWhat happensAmountPrincipalUnpaid interest
15 Jun 2025Lent60,00060,0000
15 Jul 2025Interest: 2.5% of 60,0001,50060,0001,500
15 Jul 2025Capitalised1,50061,5000

Compounded Monthly capitalises on every Accrual Date, so the ₹1,500 becomes Principal at once. The next month is charged on ₹61,500: 2.5% is ₹1,537.50, which the app rounds to the rupee, ₹1,538. Arjun paid once (₹3,000, in August 2025) and never again. Fifteen months of interest on interest later, the story has him owing ₹85,184 on a ₹60,000 ring.

Check it: The Gold Chain Story, chapter 7 ("Meanwhile, Arjun").

The "nothing" Part-Month Rule: Kavita's anklet

Kavita's anklet was lent at ₹15,000 on 1 Jul 2026, on 2% a month, Simple, with the Part-Month Rule nothing. Its month's interest is ₹300, added on the 1st.

Predict: on 20 Jul 2026, before any interest has been added, what is the anklet's Interest to Date under each of the three Part-Month Rules?

1 Jul through 20 Jul is 20 days, both ends counted. Under nothing, the anklet owes just its Principal until 1 Aug, when the month's ₹300 is added as usual. The rule changes only the month under way; it never lets a whole month off.

A Mortgage owes interest from its first day

The Interest to Date counts today, so a new Mortgage already owes something on the day it is created. Lent ₹20,000 today on Meera's Terms (pro-rata), it owes one day of 30 of the ₹400 month: ₹13, so ₹20,013. Under full month it would owe the whole first month at once, ₹20,400. (ADR 0011)

Check it: The Gold Chain Story, chapter 3, where the Lender enters a ₹20,000 Mortgage.

Paying Principal mid-month helps only from next month

A month's interest is charged on the Principal as it stood when that month began, not on the Accrual Date itself. Suppose that on 9 Oct, the day before an Accrual Date, Meera had paid off all her interest and ₹40,000 of Principal as well. The interest added on 10 Oct is still ₹1,600, because that month began on 10 Sep with ₹80,000. Only the month starting 10 Oct is charged on ₹40,000 (₹800, added on 10 Nov).

ADR 0006 explains why: charging on the Principal at the Accrual Date would let a Customer avoid almost a whole month's interest by paying a day early. "A month started is a month charged."

Accrual Dates on the 31st

A Mortgage started on 31 Jan accrues on the 31st, but February has no 31st. The Accrual Date is then clamped to the month's last day: 28 Feb (or 29th), then back to 31 Mar.

Quiz

Farida borrowed ₹30,000 on Meera's Terms on 12 May. What interest is added on 12 June?

₹600. The rate is per month: 2% of ₹30,000. ₹50 reads the rate as yearly, which it never is. ₹612 compounds, but Meera's Terms are Compounded Yearly, so nothing is capitalised until the first anniversary, and there is nothing to compound in the first month anyway.

Which of these is part of Outstanding on a day between two Accrual Dates?

The Interest to Date. Outstanding is Principal + unpaid interest + the Interest to Date, today included. Future months aren't owed yet. Leaving out the Interest to Date was the old rule ADR 0011 retired, because it made the figure a Customer was told differ from what they had to pay.

Under Simple interest, a year of unpaid interest has piled up. What happens on the anniversary?

Nothing. Simple never capitalises: interest never earns interest. Only Compounded Yearly (every 12 months) and Compounded Monthly (every month) capitalise. Interest is never written off by the passage of time.
