Chapter 009 · Lesson 1

The Call List: who to phone today, and why

On 19 Oct the Call List has two rows, both Arjun's. Why those two, and why in that order? And why isn't Meera there, when she is two months behind on her chain?

By the end you can say why any Mortgage is or isn't on today's Call List, and where it sits.

The problem

The Lender has a phone and a morning. They can't ring every Customer every day, and they shouldn't have to scan every Mortgage to decide who to ring. They want a short list, most urgent first, with the reason next to each name.

Three things make a Mortgage worth a call:

  • The Customer has stopped paying the interest: several months of it are still unpaid.
  • The jewellery is barely worth what's owed. If the Customer never comes back, the Lender loses money.
  • Interest is added today. That's a natural moment for a friendly reminder.

The obvious design is a table of "people to call", updated whenever something happens. It fails the same way a stored balance would (trap #4). Arjun pays this afternoon, a Payment from last month is back-dated, a Gold Rate moves. Each of these changes who belongs on the list, and every change would have to remember to update it.

The idea

The Call List is worked out whenever it's asked for, from the replay, like the Ledger (ADR 0001). Only three things are stored: two limits the Lender sets, and the Call Notes. Each Active Mortgage is checked against three reasons. If it has any, it's listed, carrying every reason it has.

Reason (Reason)Listed whenLimit in the sample book
months_unpaidMonths Unpaid is at or above the limit3
loan_to_valueLoan-to-Value is above the limit85%
accrues_todayToday is one of its Accrual Dates—

Then the list is sorted by urgency and grouped by Customer, so the Lender rings each person once and talks about all their Mortgages.

The flow

Notice that nothing here reads a stored figure. Months Unpaid, Outstanding and Loan-to-Value all come from replaying the Mortgage today.

The code

1. Two limits, and the Loan-to-Value test

  1. A Mortgage with no Collateral Value (no Gold Rate for its purity, chapter 008) has no Loan-to-Value, so this reason can't apply to it.
  2. >, not >=. A Mortgage at exactly 85% is not listed. Months Unpaid uses >= (below), so the two limits read differently: "at 3 months" but "above 85%".

The limits are stored as two rows in the config table, call_list_months_unpaid and call_list_loan_to_value_pct. ConfigGroup.prefixed reads and writes them as one Pydantic model (chapter 010, lesson 3 covers ConfigGroup). Field(ge=1) means "greater than or equal to 1", so PUT /api/settings/call-list with a Months Unpaid of 0 is a 422.

2. Checking each Mortgage

  1. A Dormant Mortgage is Active but owes nothing (chapter 005). Phoning about it makes no sense; what it needs is Redeeming, so it shows on the Closures page instead (ADR 0012).
  2. months_unpaid lives in lending/ledger.py (line 462) and is chapter 004's. This module only compares its answer with the limit.
  3. "Accrues today" means the Ledger has an interest step dated today. any(…) is true if at least one step matches.

3. Called today

Only notes dated today count. Building a dict from notes in id order keeps the last one per Mortgage, because a later key overwrites an earlier one. A Call Note doesn't take a Mortgage off the list. It marks the row as called, which moves it down.

4. Urgency, then one group per Customer

  1. Python sorts tuples element by element, and False sorts before True. So every row not yet called today comes before every called row, whatever its figures.
  2. Negating a number turns an ascending sort into "largest first".
  3. setdefault returns the Customer's group, creating it the first time. Python dicts keep insertion order, so the groups come out in the order of each Customer's most urgent row, and each group's rows stay in urgency order.

The route, GET /api/call-list (line 190), pages the result by whole groups: rows_page never splits a Customer across pages, and mortgages counts the rows on every page (the Dashboard shows that number). With sort= (customer, mortgage, why or outstanding) it re-sorts each group's rows by that column, then the groups by their first row.

Why isn't Meera listed?

Meera missed April's ₹1,600 on her chain. It's tempting to say "one month unpaid". The code says two, and neither of them is April. months_unpaid matches Payments to the oldest unpaid interest first, so each later Payment quietly covers the month before it:

Two is below the limit of 3, so Meera isn't listed on 19 Oct. A part-paid month still counts as unpaid. That's why September counts.

(d) Meera joins on 10 Nov

On 10 Nov the chain adds November's interest with nothing paid since September. Served on that day, the Call List has Arjun (the ring, 106.8%) and then Meera, whose chain is listed for months_unpaid (now 3) and accrues_today. (From scripts/run_throughs.py, section L1 (f).)

(e) The Dormant anklet

Kavita's anklet is Active but owes ₹0. Even on its Accrual Date (the 1st) it would never be listed, because line 131 skips it before any reason is checked. It can't be "worth a call" for Loan-to-Value either: with ₹0 owed its Loan-to-Value is 0%. A Dormant Mortgage needs the Lender to Redeem it, not phone about it (ADR 0012).

Quiz

A Mortgage owes ₹85,000 against a Collateral Value of ₹1,00,000, with 1 Month Unpaid, and accrues on the 3rd. On 19 Oct, is it on the Call List?

85,000 ÷ 1,00,000 = 0.85, and loan_to_value_too_high uses a strict >. 1 Month Unpaid is below the limit of 3, and the 3rd isn't today. So no reason applies.

Arjun rings back at noon and the Lender adds a Call Note on his chain too. What's the order of his two rows now?

Both are called now, so the first key ties. The next key is Months Unpaid, most first: the ring's 15 beats the chain's 2. A Call Note never takes a row off the list. (From scripts/run_throughs.py, section L1 (b).)

Where would you look for the row that says "call Arjun today"?

There's no Call List table. Only the two limits (in config) and the Call Notes are stored. call_note records calls already made.
