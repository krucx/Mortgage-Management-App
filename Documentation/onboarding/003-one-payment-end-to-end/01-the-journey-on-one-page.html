Chapter 003 · Lesson 1

The journey on one page

Meera hands the Lender ₹6,600 by UPI on 19 Oct 2026: ₹5,000 for her bangles and ₹1,600 for her chain. Between the Lender typing it in and Meera getting a Receipt on WhatsApp, the Payment passes through every layer of the app. This lesson is the map; the next four walk it.

By the end you can name each layer a Payment crosses and the request it makes.

The problem

A bug report says "Meera's Receipt shows the wrong amount still owed". Is that the form, the Preview, the save, the figures, or the Receipt? Each lives in a different file, in a different language, sometimes in a different process. Until you've seen one Payment go all the way through, you can't say which layer to open first.

So in this chapter you make one, on your own scratch copy of the sample book, and watch it cross every layer. Each lesson stays shallow: it shows you where things happen and names the later chapter that explains how.

The idea

A Payment makes exactly four requests, and you can watch every one of them in the browser. The frontend asks what Meera owes, asks the backend to preview the Payment, saves it, then fetches its Receipt. The backend never stores what anyone owes: it works it out from the Payments every time it's asked.

The flow

Notice the Preview: it really writes the Payment, then rolls it back. That's how a Preview always agrees with what saving would do (lesson 2).

Run-through: what you're about to make

Step 1: serve a fresh book

You'll save a Payment, which changes the book, so start from a fresh scratch folder (chapter 002, lesson 4). From backend/:

export BOOK=$(mktemp -d)
uv run python ../docs/sample-book/book.py "$BOOK"
uv run python ../docs/sample-book/serve.py "$BOOK"      # http://127.0.0.1:8765

If you haven't built the screens since you last pulled, run npm run build in frontend/ first.

Step 2: look at one Meera already made

On 10 Sep 2026 Meera paid ₹12,000 by UPI: ₹2,000 to her chain and ₹10,000 to her bangles. One Payment, two Allocations. Open it at 127.0.0.1:8765/payments/23.

From scripts/run_throughs.py, which builds the sample book and reads GET /api/payments/23, at commit 361c7fa.

The new Payment has the same shape: one Customer, one date, one method, one amount shared between two Mortgages.

Step 3: open the Network tab

Open your browser's developer tools (F12, or ⌥⌘I on a Mac), choose Network, and type /api/ in its filter box so only the backend's requests show. Keep it open for the whole chapter. Then go to 127.0.0.1:8765/payments/new?customer=1 (Meera is Customer 1; the Payments screen's Record a Payment button gets you there too, via a Customer picker).

Predict: the form opens with today's date. Which "today": the served book's 19 Oct 2026, or your computer's?

Your computer's. The form's date comes from todayIso() in frontend/src/format.ts, which reads the browser's clock. Only the backend's clock is held. So the first request asks for Meera's Mortgages as_of your real date, and the backend, which thinks it's 19 Oct 2026, refuses it:

GET /api/mortgages?…&as_of=2026-11-04&paged=false   → 422  "The date cannot be in the future"

Seen on the served book with a browser dated 4 Nov 2026.

Set Date received to 19 Oct 2026. The list request goes again, and this time it succeeds. Meera's two Active Mortgages appear, each with its Outstanding on that date. You'll keep this date for the whole chapter.

Step 4: watch the four requests

Fill in the form as lesson 2 describes, save it, and you'll see this in the Network tab:

A few other requests appear right after request 3. That's the app refreshing what the Payment made stale, and lesson 5 explains it.

Quiz

Which of the four requests writes rows that stay in the database?

Only the save keeps its rows. The Preview writes the same rows and then rolls them back. The Receipt only reads.

The Lender wants to send the Receipt on WhatsApp. Which screen has the button?

The Payment's page shows the Receipt's WhatsApp text and a Send on WhatsApp button. The /receipt page is for printing or saving as PDF, and the form leaves for the Receipt once it has saved.

The first request fails with "The date cannot be in the future". Why?

The form starts at the browser's date; serve.py holds the backend on 19 Oct 2026, so your real date is "in the future" to it. Meera's Mortgages exist, and the served clock never moves.
