Chapter 003 · Lesson 4

What the Ledger says back

The save answered with Meera's Payment, and it knows something the request never said: of the bangles' ₹5,000, ₹1,500 paid interest and ₹3,500 paid Principal. The tables from lesson 3 have no column for that. So where did it come from?

By the end you can explain why nothing owed is stored, and where the interest/Principal division comes from.

The problem

How a Payment divides between interest and Principal depends on what was owed when it arrived. That depends on every earlier Payment, the Mortgage's Terms, and the dates. If the app stored "₹1,500 interest, ₹3,500 Principal" on the Payment, any later correction would quietly make it wrong: a back-dated Payment, a Void, a Term Change. Chapter 001 called this the one big idea; here it is in code.

The idea

Store only the facts: a Payment, its date, and how much went to each Mortgage. Whenever anyone asks how a Payment was applied, or what a Mortgage owes, Replay the Mortgage from its start: rebuild its Ledger step by step and read the answer off it. A few hundred Mortgages replay in milliseconds (ADR 0001).

Nuances

(a) The division can change after it's saved

Predict: after saving Payment 34, the Lender finds a slip of paper: Meera also paid ₹2,000 on the chain on 15 Oct, and records it now. Payment 34's stored rows don't change. Does the chain's line on Payment 34 still say "₹1,600 to interest, ₹0 to Principal"?

Payment 34, chainStored AllocationTo interestTo Principal
Before the 15 Oct Payment₹1,600₹1,600₹0
After it₹1,600 (row unchanged)₹1,333₹267

From scripts/run_throughs.py (step 5b), which records the 15 Oct Payment, re-reads Payment 34 and its allocation rows, then Voids the 15 Oct Payment again.

The 15 Oct ₹2,000 now comes first in the replay and pays ₹2,000 of the ₹2,800 unpaid. On 19 Oct, Payment 34's ₹1,600 finds only ₹800 unpaid, then ₹533 of Interest to Date, and pays the last ₹267 off the Principal. Nobody rewrote Payment 34. The replay simply saw a different history. This is intended, not a bug: it's why corrections are safe. Chapter 004 owns the details.

Voiding the 15 Oct Payment puts Payment 34 back to ₹1,600 / ₹0, for the same reason.

Newcomer trap #4: nothing owed is stored. There's no ledger table, no stored Outstanding, no stored interest-paid. Don't add one "for speed"; you'd reintroduce exactly the bug ADR 0001 removed. See the traps sheet.

(b) Paying exactly what's owed closes the Mortgage

Lesson 2's nuance (c) showed it in the Preview: an Allocation equal to the Mortgage's whole Outstanding on its date Redeems it, in the same request (ADR 0011). That's the branch of record_payment Meera's Payment skipped. A whole Outstanding that can't Redeem is refused instead. Chapter 005 explains Redemption and when it's refused.

Quiz

Where is "₹3,500 to Principal" for the bangles stored?

The allocation row holds only the ₹5,000. History records the Payment's fields, not how it was applied. load_payment replays the bangles and reads principal_paid off the Payment's Ledger step.

A Payment is Voided. What does load_payment return for its interest and Principal paid?

A Voided Payment is excluded from every replay (voided_at.is_(None)), so there's no Ledger step to read. Only its stored amounts remain.

What does compute_ledger need from the database?

It's a pure function. replays does the reading (two queries) and hands it dates, amounts and Terms. There are no Ledger rows to write.
