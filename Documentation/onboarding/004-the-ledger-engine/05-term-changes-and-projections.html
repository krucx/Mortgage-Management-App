Chapter 004 · Lesson 5

Term Changes and Projections

On 12 Oct the Lender agreed to drop Meera's bangles from 2% to 1.5% a month, from their next Accrual Date, 25 Oct. On the served book it's 19 Oct, so the change hasn't bitten yet. Meera asks two things: "When does it start to count?" and "What will I owe in a year?" Both answers come from the replay you already know.

By the end you can say when a Term Change starts to bite, and read a Projection.

The problem

The simple way to change a rate is to update the Mortgage's monthly_rate_bps column. But a Replay starts from the start date every time (lesson 1). With the rate overwritten, every month since April would be charged at 1.5%. Interest that Meera has already paid, and Receipts she has already been sent, would all change. The Terms need a history: which Terms held for which month.

The question about next year has a trap too. Working out next year with a separate "forecast" formula would mean a second copy of the rules: Accrual Dates, Capitalisation, the coming Term Change. Sooner or later the two copies would disagree.

The idea

A Term Change is one more stored fact: a date, which must be an Accrual Date, and only the fields that change. Each month is charged under the Terms in force on the day it starts. A month that started before 25 Oct keeps the old Terms, even though its interest is added on 25 Oct.

A Projection is the same replay, continued past today, with the coming Term Changes and, if you ask, made-up monthly Payments. There is no separate maths.

The flow

Notice that saving the change on 12 Oct changes nothing until a month starts under it. Nothing runs on 25 Oct either: the replay just passes that date.

The code

1. A Term Change, and the Terms for a month

  1. Meera's change is TermChange(on=25 Oct, monthly_rate_bps=150). Her Interest Mode and Part-Month Rule carry over from the Terms in force then.
  2. Changes are applied one by one, in date order, up to day. The original Terms on the mortgage row are never overwritten.

2. Where the engine asks for Terms

  1. The month ending on on began on the previous Accrual Date (n - 1). Its Terms are looked up for that day. On 25 Oct that's 25 Sep: 2%.
  2. Whether to capitalise also follows the old month's Terms.
  3. A Projection's made-up Payment (more below). It comes after the day's real Payments (line 282) and before Capitalisation. payment_id is None, which is how you tell it apart.
  4. If the month starting today has different Terms, a term_changed step marks it, after everything else that day.

3. A Projection: the replay, past today

  1. The same _Run as a Ledger: same interest, same order inside a day, same Capitalisation.
  2. One replay, stopping at each date to take a Snapshot of where the Mortgage stands. It never starts again, so 13 dates cost one replay, not 13.
  3. To answer "when would it be paid off?", it keeps going (up to ten years) until a made-up Payment leaves nothing owed (paid_off_on, set in pay).

The route is GET /api/mortgages/{id}/projection, in lending/projections.py (line 111). It asks for today and each month for a year (accrual_date(today, months), so the 19th of each month on the book). It passes HypotheticalPayments(after=today, amount=monthly_payment) when you type a monthly figure. It reaches project through MortgageReplay.projection (replay.py line 246). baseline (line 100) is the "no more Payments" series on its own. Chapter 012 opens the Dashboard, which uses it.

Nuances

(a) A Term Change on an Accrual Date keeps that day on the old Terms

Predict: suppose Meera's chain (Compounded Yearly) had a Term Change to Simple from 10 Nov 2026, its first anniversary. Is the ₹4,400 still capitalised on 10 Nov?

Yes. The year that ends on 10 Nov ran under Compounded Yearly, and _capitalises is asked with that month's Terms (line 278). The change applies to the months starting 10 Nov. Nothing earlier changes, as the glossary promises: "it never rewrites earlier interest". Test: test_a_switch_from_simple_to_compounded_monthly pins the mirror image: a switch on 14 Apr doesn't capitalise on 14 Apr, but on 14 May it capitalises all the Simple months' interest.

(b) Made-up Payments come after the real ones

The first made-up Payment falls on the first Accrual Date after today: 25 Oct. If Meera had also really paid ₹5,000 on 25 Oct, the real Payment comes first. It pays the ₹2,000 unpaid, ₹75 ahead (1 day at 1.5%) and ₹2,925 of Principal. Then the made-up ₹10,000 all goes to Principal. Each made-up Payment is capped at what's owed (line 286), so the last one is smaller and leaves exactly nothing. Test: test_hypothetical_payments_fall_on_each_accrual_date_until_paid_off.

(c) A Projection is not a Trend

The Dashboard also shows Trends: the last 3 full months' average carried forward (lending/dashboard.py). A Trend is an estimate and replays nothing. A Projection is exact under its assumptions, because it's the real engine. Don't call either a "forecast": that's on both terms' Avoid lists. Chapter 012 owns the Dashboard.

(d) A Term Change has to start on an Accrual Date

The engine assumes it (TermChange's docstring). The route that saves one, in lending/changes.py, refuses any other date: "A Term Change starts on an Accrual Date, today or later". Chapter 006 owns that route and its Preview.

Trap #8, a warning: there's no scheduler. Nothing "applies" the Term Change on 25 Oct; the replay simply reaches that date. The daily jobs that do exist (Backups, Notices) run on the first /api/ request of the day, from middleware. Chapter 010 owns that; see the traps sheet.

Quiz

A Mortgage accrues on the 5th. A Term Change to 1% starts on 5 Mar. Which is the first interest charged at 1%?

The interest added on 5 Mar is for the month that started on 5 Feb, under the old Terms. The month starting 5 Mar is the first under 1%, and its interest is added on 5 Apr. The Interest to Date from 5 Mar on is at 1% too.

How does a Projection work out next year's interest?

project builds a _Run and calls accrue_through, exactly like compute_ledger. projections.py only picks the dates and shapes the answer. Averaging is a Trend.

In a Projection's Ledger, how do you tell a made-up Payment from a real one?

Both are "payment" steps. A made-up one is applied with self.pay(on, …, None), so it has no Payment id (LedgerStep.payment_id's comment says so). The last one is capped at what's owed, so it needn't be round.
