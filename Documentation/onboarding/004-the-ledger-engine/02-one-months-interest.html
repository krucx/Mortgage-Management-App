Chapter 004 · Lesson 2

One month's interest

"2% a month" fits on a sticky note. Turning it into rupees means deciding which day it's added, in what unit it's counted, how it's rounded, what a short month does, and what a part of a month costs. This lesson reads the four small functions that decide all of that.

By the end you can work out any month's interest charge, and any Interest to Date, by hand from a Mortgage's Terms.

The problem

Here are the questions the code has to answer, each with a wrong answer that looks fine at first:

QuestionTempting answerWhy it fails
What unit is money in?Rupees as a float0.1 + 0.2 != 0.3 in floating point. Sums of thousands of Payments drift by paise.
What's 2.5% of ₹61,500?₹1,537.50The Lender charges whole rupees. Rounding has to be one fixed rule, written once.
A Mortgage started on 31 Jan. When is February's interest?31 FebThere is no 31 Feb. Python's date(2026, 2, 31) raises an error.
What's 10 days of a month worth?10 ÷ days in that monthThen a day in February costs more than a day in March. The Lender wants every month to count the same.

The idea

Every money figure is an integer number of paise. A rate is an integer number of basis points per month (1 basis point = 0.01%, so 200 = 2% a month). One function, monthly_interest, turns a Principal and a rate into a charge for a month, or for some days of a month counted out of 30. It rounds half-up to the whole rupee. The Accrual Date is the start date's day of the month, moved back to the month's last day when the month is too short.

The flow

Notice that there's only one formula. The Part-Month Rule only decides how many days to give it.

The code

1. Terms: three fields

A StrEnum is an enum whose members are also plain strings, so InterestMode.SIMPLE == "simple". That's what the API sends and the database stores. Terms is a Pydantic model: Field(gt=0, le=10_000) refuses a rate of zero or above 100% a month. The Interest Mode is lesson 4's. This lesson uses the other two fields.

2. Accrual Dates and the month-end clamp

  1. Counts months from January of the start year, then splits that count back into a year and a month. // is whole-number division and % the remainder.
  2. calendar.monthrange(year, month)[1] is the number of days in that month. Taking the smaller of it and the start day is the clamp: 31 becomes 28 in February.
  3. The start date itself is Accrual Date number 0, not an Accrual Date: months >= 1.

Every Accrual Date is worked out from the start, never from the one before. So a short month doesn't drag the rest of the year earlier. A Mortgage started on 31 Jan 2026 accrues on 28 Feb, 31 Mar, 30 Apr, 31 May.

3. monthly_interest: the one formula

  1. The charge in rupees is principal_paise × bps × days ÷ (10,000 × 30 × 100): ÷10,000 turns basis points into a fraction, ÷30 turns days into a fraction of a month, ÷100 turns paise into rupees.
  2. Integer rounding half-up. (2n + d) // (2d) is the same as floor(n/d + ½), but uses whole numbers only, so it's exact. Python's own round() rounds halves to the nearest even number (round(2.5) == 2), which is why it isn't used.
  3. Back to paise, always a whole rupee.

4. Part of a month: the Part-Month Rules

  1. The + 1 is "today always counts". On an Accrual Date itself that's 1 day of the new month.
  2. match … case (Python 3.10+) compares the value against each case in turn, like a switch.
  3. A 31-day month's last day would be day 31. min(…, 30) caps it, so the Interest to Date never exceeds a whole month's charge.

Nuances

(a) Rounding half-up

Predict: Arjun's ring is at 2.5% a month. After its first Capitalisation the Principal was ₹61,500, and 2.5% of that is ₹1,537.50 exactly. What does the Ledger charge? And on ₹61,540 (exactly ₹1,538.50)?

From scripts/run_throughs.py (section L2).

Halves always go up. Python's round() would give 1,538 for both halves (it rounds to even), which is why the code doesn't use it. You can see the ₹1,538 on the ring's complete Ledger (/mortgages/1): "Interest: 2.5% of ₹61,500" on 15 Aug 2025.

(b) A start on the 31st

The clamp happens one month at a time: accrual_date(31 Jan 2026, n) gives 28 Feb, 31 Mar, 30 Apr, 31 May. For a start on 31 Oct 2026 it gives 30 Nov, 31 Dec, 31 Jan, 28 Feb. The test test_accrual_dates_fall_on_the_start_day_or_month_end pins the same pattern for a start on 31 Jan 2024, a leap year: 29 Feb, then back to 31 Mar, not the 29th.

(c) Kavita's anklet and the "nothing" rule

Predict: the anklet's Part-Month Rule is "nothing". On 19 Oct, 19 days into its month, what is its Interest to Date, and what is the last step of its Ledger?

₹0, and there's no interest_to_date step at all. The last step is 1 Oct's interest. charge_interest_to_date (line 322) adds the step only when there's something to show. Under "nothing", a Customer who settles mid-month pays nothing for the part month. Under "full month" they'd pay the whole month from its first day: ₹1,600 for Meera's chain, even on day 1.

(d) Default Terms: the book is not a fresh database

The sample book's Default Terms are 2%, Compounded Yearly, pro-rata, set by book.py. A fresh database starts at 3%, Compounded Yearly, full month (migration 0001; see 002 · Lesson 4). On a fresh database, the chain would be charged ₹2,400 a month, and ₹2,400 on day 1 of each month as its Interest to Date. When a test's figures surprise you, check which Terms it set up first.

Newcomer trap #6: units. Money is paise (integers): 160000 is ₹1,600. Rates are basis points per month: 200 is 2% a month, not a year. Pro-rata counts days out of 30, whatever the month. And ₹1,00,000 (one lakh) is Indian digit grouping for a hundred thousand. Weights are milligrams (chapter 008). This chapter owns trap #6; see the traps sheet.

Quiz

A ₹50,000 Mortgage at 300 bps, pro-rata. What is its Interest to Date 7 days into a month?

3% of ₹50,000 is ₹1,500 a month. Pro-rata counts days out of 30 in every month: 7/30 × ₹1,500 = ₹350. "Full month" would give ₹1,500. No rule uses the month's real length.

A Mortgage started on 30 Jan 2026. When is its second Accrual Date?

The first is 28 Feb (clamped). But each Accrual Date is accrual_date(start, n), worked out from the start date, so the second is 30 Mar. Nothing is counted in 30-day steps.

In the API, what does "monthly_rate_bps": 150 mean?

Basis points per month: 150 bps = 1.50% a month. It's the rate Meera's bangles change to in lesson 5. Trap #6.
