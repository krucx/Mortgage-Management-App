Chapter 004 · Lesson 4

Capitalisation

Arjun borrowed ₹60,000 on his ruby ring and has paid ₹3,000 since. Sixteen months later he owes ₹85,184. On Simple interest he would owe ₹81,250. The ₹3,934 between the two is Capitalisation: unpaid interest that joined the Principal and started earning interest itself. This lesson shows exactly when that happens, and what a Customer can pay to stop it.

By the end you can say when unpaid interest joins the Principal, and how a Customer avoids it.

The problem

Interest that's charged and not paid sits as unpaid interest. If it sits for ever, the Lender is in effect lending that interest for free. So some Terms say that, at the end of a compounding period, unpaid interest becomes Principal: Capitalisation. The Interest Mode sets the period: never (Simple), every twelfth Accrual Date (Compounded Yearly), or every Accrual Date (Compounded Monthly).

The code has to settle three questions. On which Accrual Dates does it happen? Does it happen before or after that day's Payments? And does interest a Customer already paid ahead count as unpaid?

The idea

On an Accrual Date where the Interest Mode says so, after that day's interest and that day's Payments, whatever is still unpaid moves into the Principal. It's also added to the new month's starting Principal, so the month that began that day is charged on it. Interest paid ahead was never unpaid, so it can't be capitalised. The Interest to Date is never capitalised either.

So the way to avoid Capitalisation is to pay all unpaid interest by the end of the Accrual Date. Paying on the day itself is enough.

The flow

Notice the two += lines. The new month has already started on the old Principal, so the capitalised amount is added to its starting Principal as well.

The code

1. Which Accrual Dates

n is the Accrual Date's number, counted from the start date. So "yearly" means each anniversary of the start date, not 31 March or 1 January. Meera's chain started on 10 Nov 2025, so its first Capitalisation can only be on 10 Nov 2026.

2. When, and how much

  1. Decided on the Terms of the month just ended (month_terms). That matters when a Term Change starts today (lesson 5).
  2. Today's Payments go first. A Payment made today can still clear the interest and stop it being capitalised.
  3. self.unpaid holds only interest that was charged and not paid. What a Payment paid ahead was subtracted at line 266 and never reached it.
  4. The month that began at line 280 is now charged on the bigger Principal. The Interest to Date step at the end of the Ledger shows this: charged_on is the capitalised Principal.

Three things to notice:

  • On 14 Aug, 30 days into the month, his ₹3,000 found nothing unpaid (July's interest had been capitalised). So it paid the Interest to Date ahead, ₹1,538, and the rest went to Principal. On 15 Aug the month's ₹1,538 was all paid ahead, and there was nothing to capitalise.

  • On 19 Oct he owes ₹84,831 + ₹353 = ₹85,184, with 15 Capitalisations in his Ledger. Had the ring been Simple, he'd owe ₹60,000 + ₹21,250 = ₹81,250.

(c) Every month, on the ring

Compounded Monthly gives Arjun the same choice every month. On 15 Sep 2025, ₹1,000 that day leaves ₹501 to be capitalised. ₹1,550 that day pays all ₹1,501 and ₹49 of the new month ahead, so nothing is capitalised. A Customer on Compounded Monthly who pays each month's interest by its Accrual Date never sees a Capitalisation.

(d) The Interest to Date is never capitalised

Only self.unpaid is capitalised, and on an open Mortgage the Interest to Date only joins it at the very end of the Ledger, after every Accrual Date (lesson 3). The ring's ₹353 on 19 Oct will never be capitalised as it stands. On 15 Nov it's replaced by November's full month (ADR 0011).

Quiz

A Compounded Yearly Mortgage started on 3 Feb 2025. When is its first Capitalisation, if interest is unpaid?

_capitalises tests n % 12 == 0, counting Accrual Dates from the start. The calendar and the financial year play no part.

On a Compounded Monthly Mortgage, ₹900 is unpaid after an Accrual Date's interest. ₹600 is paid that same day. What is capitalised?

Whether to capitalise is decided before the Payments; how much is what's unpaid after them (line 288). ₹900 − ₹600 = ₹300.

After a Capitalisation, why does line 291 add to month_start_principal?

Line 280 started the new month on the Principal before that day's Payments and Capitalisation. Without line 291, the month would be charged as if nothing had been capitalised. The month just ended was already charged at line 265.
