Chapter 005 · Lesson 2

Closing on a date

Before any Closure is recorded, the Lender needs a figure: "if you close it on this date, it takes this much". This lesson follows that quote from the API route down to the lines in compute_ledger that close a Mortgage, and shows the three reasons a close is refused.

By the end you can quote what closing would take on a date, and say why a close is refused.

The problem

Farida Khan borrowed ₹30,000 against a gold nose ring on 12 May 2026, on the sample book's Default Terms: ₹600 of interest on each 12th. She paid ₹600 every month from June to September. On 16 Oct she came in to settle. The Lender opened the Mortgage page, chose Close this Mortgage, and picked a date. The form showed the Outstanding on that date before anything was saved.

Two things make that quote harder than lesson 1's outstanding(on):

  • The date may not be today. The Lender may record yesterday's visit late, or tell a Customer what Friday will cost. The quote has to be right for any date.
  • Some dates make no sense. Closing before the Mortgage started, or before a Payment already recorded on it, would leave the book showing money paid on a Mortgage that had already ended.

The idea

Replay the Mortgage as if it closed on that date: stop there, fix that day's Interest to Date, and read the totals. That's outstanding_on_closure(on). Before quoting or closing, one guard, closable, refuses a date that can't be a Closure date.

The flow

Notice that the status check comes first. A closed Mortgage is refused for being closed, whatever date you ask about.

The code

1. The route

  1. engine.connect(), not engine.begin(): a read, with no transaction to commit. EngineDep and TodayDep are FastAPI dependencies; today is the app's clock, held on 19 Oct 2026 for the sample book (chapter 010 owns the clock).

2. The guard: closable and not_closable

  1. Already closed. Undoing that is a Reopen (lesson 5).
  2. Before the Mortgage existed.
  3. Any non-Voided Allocation dated after on. Strictly after: a Payment on the Closure date itself is fine. max(…, default=None) gives None instead of raising when there are none; the message names the latest, which is the earliest date that would work.

Why return a message instead of raising? Because record_payment asks the same question about a Payment that would Redeem (lesson 3), and the bulk Closures add the Mortgage's name. Each caller decides how to refuse.

3. The quote: outstanding_on_closure

  1. Leave out the Closure's own Final Payment, if it already has one. Otherwise, asked about a Mortgage that's already Redeemed, the quote would count the Payment that paid it against itself and come to ₹0. The check-yourself exercise breaks exactly this line.
  2. closed_on=on with no closure kind: "a quote of closing then", in compute_ledger's words.
  3. next(generator, None) returns the first step that matches, or None. The Interest to Date is split out of the unpaid interest so the form can show it on its own line.
  4. The total is the same sum lesson 1's outstanding takes: the last step's Principal plus unpaid interest.

4. Inside the engine: stopping at a Closure

  1. A closed Mortgage never replays past its Closure, whatever as_of you ask for. That's why lesson 1's necklace owed nothing on 19 Oct.
  2. The Final Payment is held back, but only on the closing day itself.
  3. Every other Payment that day is applied first, as on any day (chapter 004, lesson 3).
  4. The Interest to Date on the Closure date is charged into the unpaid interest. charge_interest_to_date also sets to_date_charged_on, so the Final Payment isn't asked to pay it ahead a second time.
  5. Then the Final Payment, which pays the unpaid interest (Interest to Date included) and then Principal.

The 10 Nov quote is the chain's first anniversary: chapter 004, lesson 4's ₹4,400 has been capitalised into the Principal. A quote runs the whole engine, so it's right about the future too, as long as no more Payments come. A Closure can't be recorded for a future date, because it says the jewellery has gone back or been sold. That hasn't happened yet.

From scripts/run_throughs.py (section L2): the quote's replay with an extra Allocation of ₹100 on 16 Oct.

₹30,600. The ₹100 is applied by pay_through(as_of) at line 398, before the Interest to Date is fixed at line 402. It pays October's unpaid interest first, as any Payment does. Only the Closure's own Final Payment waits until after the Interest to Date. That's what makes "pay the whole quote" always close the Mortgage exactly.

(d) The Interest to Date is fixed at the Closure

On the served book, Farida's Ledger on 19 Oct ends on 16 Oct: interest_to_date ₹100 (5 days), then her Payment, then the Waiver (lesson 4). The Mortgage page's Interest to Date line says "its Closure included" instead of "today included". Ask for the Ledger on any later date and line 380 clamps it back to 16 Oct, so those 5 days never become 6.

Quiz

Meera's chain has Payments on 10 Sep and earlier. Can it be closed on 10 Sep itself?

not_closable looks for a.on > on, strictly after. A Payment on the Closure date is allowed, and is applied before the Interest to Date is fixed.

Today is 19 Oct. The Lender asks what closing the chain on 10 Nov would take. What happens?

The quote route passes quote_only=True, which skips the future-date check. The engine replays to 10 Nov, capitalising on the anniversary. Only recording a Closure on 10 Nov is refused.

Why does the Final Payment wait until after the Interest to Date is charged?

Every other Payment comes first, then the Interest to Date is fixed, then the Final Payment pays everything left: interest first, then Principal. A Final Payment of the quote leaves exactly ₹0.
