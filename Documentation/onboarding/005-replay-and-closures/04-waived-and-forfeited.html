Chapter 005 · Lesson 4

Waived and Forfeited

Farida came in ₹180 short and the Lender let it off. Imran never came back and his earrings were sold. Both Mortgages must end owing nothing, and both records must still say truthfully what happened to the money. This lesson shows how one extra Ledger step does both.

By the end you can say what a Waiver and a Forfeiture write off, and read a Closure.

The problem

A Redemption is simple: the Final Payment covers the Outstanding and the Ledger reaches ₹0 by itself. The other two Closures don't.

  • Waived: Farida owed ₹30,700 on 16 Oct and paid ₹30,520. The jewellery went back anyway. After her Payment, the Ledger still shows ₹180 owed.
  • Forfeited: Imran owed ₹62,840 on 10 Oct and paid nothing. The Lender sold the earrings for ₹52,000. No Payment came from Imran at all.

If the Ledger just stopped there, the closed Mortgage would show ₹180, or ₹62,840, owed forever. It would appear in totals, and the screens would ask for money nobody will pay. If instead the app recorded a made-up Payment of ₹180 or ₹62,840 to reach zero, the book would claim cash that never came in.

The idea

End the Closure's Ledger with one more step that writes off whatever is still owed: "waived" for a Waiver, "forfeited" for a Forfeiture. It moves no money; it records a decision. What it came to (the Waived Amount, the Lender's profit or loss) isn't stored. It's read off the replay.

The flow

Notice where waived_amount and profit_loss appear: only at the very bottom, after the replay. Neither is a column.

The code

1. waive: a Final Payment short of the quote

  1. Nothing to let off: that's a Redemption with no Final Payment.
  2. Paying all of it is a Redemption too (chapter 001 asked you to predict this). A Waiver always lets something off.
  3. The same function as a Redemption, with a different status and a note. An amount_paid of 0 writes no Payment, as lesson 3 showed: the Lender can let off everything.

2. forfeit: no Payment at all

The jewellery's sale isn't a Payment from the Customer, so nothing goes in the payment table. The Recovered Amount sits on the mortgage row, and the jewellery settles the debt.

3. The write-off step

LedgerStep(on, event, amount, principal, unpaid_interest): the step's amount is everything still owed after the Final Payment, and its running totals are 0 and 0. So lesson 1's outstanding(), which reads the last step, gives ₹0 with no special case for closed Mortgages.

4. Reading it back: Closure

  1. What it owed on the Closure date, before its Final Payment. This is why the quote leaves the Final Payment out.
  2. The Ledger's last step on the Closure date.
  3. The Waived Amount is the write-off step's amount.
  4. Profit or loss: Recovered Amount less the Outstanding on the Closure date. Negative is a loss.

Only kind, on, payment_id, recovered_amount and note come from columns. Everything else is replayed. When ADR 0011 changed how the Interest to Date counts days, old Waived Amounts and Forfeiture profits moved by up to a day's interest (or a month's, under full month), and the ADR accepted that. That was only possible because they aren't stored.

+₹1,76,667: 2,60,000 − 83,333. The Ledger ends with a forfeited step of ₹83,333; the chain's 20 g of 22K gold was worth far more than it owed. The app records a profit as plainly as a loss, in green on the page.

(c) owed_after ignores what came later that day

A Receipt's "still owed" line uses this (chapter 007). It answers "what did this Mortgage owe right after this Payment?", adding back anything paid or written off later on the same day. ledger[at + 1 :] is a Python slice: every step after index at.

Predict: what is owed_after(30, date(2026, 10, 16)) for Farida's Final Payment?

₹180. The Ledger ends at 0 + 0, and the waived step after Payment 30 adds back its ₹180. That's true: just after her Payment, before the Lender let it off, she owed ₹180. Her Receipt still says "Still owed ₹0", because payments/receipts.py special-cases a Waiver's Final Payment ("A Waiver's final Payment leaves nothing owed: the rest was let off with it"). For Imran's 10 Aug Payment, owed_after(19, date(2026, 8, 10)) is ₹60,440: ₹60,000 + ₹400 unpaid + ₹40, one day's Interest to Date.

(d) The bulk routes: all or none

The Closures page can Redeem, Waive or Forfeit several Mortgages at once: POST /api/closures/redeem, /waive and /forfeit, handled by redeem_all, waive_all and forfeit_all. Each loops over the single-Mortgage function inside one transaction. Asked to Forfeit Meera's chain and Farida's nose ring, it refuses with "2026-Farida-GoldNoseRing is already waived", and the chain stays Active: the whole request rolled back (from scripts/run_throughs.py, section L4).

Quiz

Where is a Waived Mortgage's Waived Amount stored?

closure() reads it off the last Ledger step on the Closure date. The History note does mention it, but History is a record of the change, never read back for figures.

A Customer owes ₹40,000 on the Closure date and the jewellery fetches ₹38,500. What is profit_loss?

Recovered Amount − Outstanding on the Closure date: 38,500 − 40,000 = −1,500. The debt is settled (the forfeited step writes off all ₹40,000), but the Lender got ₹1,500 less than was owed.

Farida brings the whole ₹30,700, and the Lender tries to Waive it with nothing let off. What happens?

waive refuses when amount_paid >= owed. A Waiver always lets something off; paying everything is a Redemption.
