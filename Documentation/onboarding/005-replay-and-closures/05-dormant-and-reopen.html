Chapter 005 · Lesson 5

Dormant, and undoing a Closure

Two ways the book can be wrong about how a Mortgage ended. Kavita's anklet owes nothing but the app still thinks it's held. And any Closure can be recorded by mistake. This lesson shows how the app flags the first without guessing, and undoes the second without losing History.

By the end you can spot a Dormant Mortgage, say why it isn't Redeemed for you, and Reopen a Closure.

The problem

On 18 Oct the Lender typed Kavita's anklet in from the paper book: ₹15,000 lent on 1 Jul, and three Payments. One slip said 25 Aug where it meant 25 Sep. Chapter 004 replayed it: the anklet now owes ₹0. It's Active and still in the Lender's safe.

The obvious fix is to Redeem it automatically whenever an Active Mortgage reaches ₹0. But the app can't know whether the anklet really went home. Here it hasn't: the book is wrong, not the safe. A Redemption would say the jewellery was returned, and the Lender would stop looking for it.

The second problem is the reverse: the Lender Redeems the wrong Mortgage, or records a Forfeiture that didn't happen. Closed Mortgages refuse Payments, Voids and edits, so the mistake can't be patched from the outside.

The idea

Dormant is a flag, worked out on every replay: Active, and owing nothing. The app lists Dormant Mortgages and leaves the decision to the Lender (ADR 0012).

Reopen undoes a Closure, with a reason. It clears the Closure columns and Voids the Final Payment, if there was one. Since every figure is replayed, the Mortgage is then exactly as if it had never been closed.

The flow

Notice that the arrows inside Active are labelled with things that happen to Payments. Nothing marks a Mortgage Dormant; it just is, whenever it's Active and owes ₹0.

The code

1. dormant()

  1. Every closed Mortgage owes ₹0 too (lesson 4's write-off steps make sure of it). Without this check, every Redeemed, Waived and Forfeited Mortgage would be Dormant.
  2. Lesson 1's Outstanding on that date, Interest to Date included. Since a Payment of the whole Outstanding Redeems (lesson 3), an Active Mortgage only reaches ₹0 when facts combine afterwards: Payments entered late or back-dated, an edit, or a Void (ADR 0012). The anklet's three Payments were each less than it owed on their own dates.

2. The Dormant list

The Closures page lists these with a red dot on its tab (chapter 012, lesson 3 owns the page). Notices and the Call List skip Dormant Mortgages the same way, by calling dormant() on their replays (chapter 009, lesson 1 and lesson 3).

3. reopen()

  1. Clear every Closure column first. The Mortgage is Active again before anything else happens.
  2. Then Void the Final Payment through the ordinary Void, with the Reopen's reason. row was read before step ① cleared it, so it still holds the old id. The order matters: void_payment refuses a Payment that is some Mortgage's redemption_payment_id, or that belongs to a closed Mortgage. After step ①, it's neither.
  3. History records the old status and the reason. The Voided Payment keeps its row, its amount and the same reason, so nothing is lost.

4. Why you can't just Void a Final Payment

Voiding a Final Payment on its own would leave a Redeemed Mortgage whose Redemption was never paid. So the app sends you to Reopen, which undoes both together. _refuse_unless_active (line 176) gives every other change on a closed Mortgage the same answer: "… is redeemed: reopen it first".

Nuances

(a) Dormant is a recording slip, not a stage of life

ADR 0012 rejected storing Dormant as a status: a stored flag "would fall out of step with the Payments it is derived from", as the Void above shows. It also rejected automatic Redemption, "because the Lender must confirm the jewellery was returned before the record says so". So Dormant should be rare and short-lived: a slip to put right by Redeeming or by correcting a Payment. Don't build features that treat it as a normal step between Active and Redeemed. It isn't the old "Ready to Redeem" either, which ADR 0011 retired.

(b) The status check is pinned

Every caller of dormant() today already passes only Active rows: the Dormant route, Notices and the Call List all select status == ACTIVE first. So deleting the check at line 164 would break nothing you can see, and for a while no test noticed. Issue #113 asked for one. Now test_a_closed_mortgage_owing_nothing_is_never_dormant in tests/test_dormant.py Redeems, Waives and Forfeits three Mortgages and asserts none is Dormant. The next caller you write may pass every Mortgage, and the check keeps it right.

(c) Reopening a Forfeiture

A Forfeiture has no Final Payment, so its Reopen Voids nothing. It clears recovered_amount and closure_note with the other columns. On a copy, Reopening Imran's earrings left them Active, owing ₹63,200 on 19 Oct: ₹60,000 + ₹2,800 + ₹400 (10 days) (from scripts/run_throughs.py, section L5).

(d) Reopening twice

Reopen an Active Mortgage and you get 409: "2026-Kavita-SilverGoldAnklet is Active". A Reopen of a Redemption that came with an ordinary Payment (lesson 3's chain and bangles) Voids only the chain's Final Payment part and leaves the bangles' part alone. That's why a Final Payment is its own part (trap #2, chapter 007, lesson 2).

Quiz

Where is a Mortgage's Dormant flag stored?

It's derived on every replay: Active and owing ₹0. ADR 0012 rejected a stored status, because Voids and corrections would leave it wrong.

The Lender wants to cancel the UPI Payment that Redeemed Kavita's coin. What should they do?

Voiding it directly is refused with 409 ("reopen the Mortgage instead, which voids it"). reopen clears the Closure first, then Voids the Final Payment with the same reason, in one transaction.

Imran's Forfeiture is Reopened. What is Voided?

redemption_payment_id is None for a Forfeiture, so void_payment isn't called. The Recovered Amount was never a Payment; it's cleared from the row. His own Payments are untouched.
