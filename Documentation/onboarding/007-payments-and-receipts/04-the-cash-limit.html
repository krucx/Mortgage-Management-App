Chapter 007 · Lesson 4

The Cash Limit

Large sums in cash are something the Lender is supposed to avoid. This lesson shows how the app spots them before they're saved, what it counts as one sum, and why it warns but never refuses.

By the end you can say what counts as one cash sum, and why the Cash Limit only warns.

The problem

Government of India rules ask for sums of ₹20,000 or more to be lent or received by UPI or bank transfer, not in cash. That's what the app tells the Lender, in useCashWarning.tsx. Meera's chain was lent ₹80,000 in cash on 10 Nov 2025: well over. Every Payment the Lender records, every Mortgage they lend, could cross that line.

Two things make it harder than "is amount ≥ ₹20,000?":

  • What is "one sum"? In lesson 2, Meera's ₹84,333 transfer became two payment rows: ₹1,000 and ₹83,333. If that had been cash, Meera would have handed over ₹84,333 in notes. Checking each row would miss the point.
  • Many changes can write cash. A Payment, but also a Redemption's Final Payment, a Waiver's, a new Mortgage lent in cash, or an edit that raises a cash Principal. A rule in each place would be a rule forgotten somewhere.

The idea

Let the Preview find the cash. Every change already has a Preview that runs the real change and rolls it back (chapter 006). Just before the change, note the highest payment id and each Mortgage's Principal and Lent Through. Just after, list every new cash sum: each new Payment, its parts added together, and each Principal now lent in cash that is new or larger. The screen compares the largest with the Cash Limit and, at or above it, asks the Lender to confirm. Saving checks nothing.

The flow

Notice the save request at the bottom goes straight through. The warning lives entirely in the Preview and the page.

The code

1. The setting: default, cleared, or set

  1. ₹20,000 in paise. Python allows _ in number literals for readability; 20_000_00 is 2000000, written so the rupees and the paise stand apart.
  2. No row in the config table at all: the Lender never set one, so the default applies.
  3. A row with an empty string: the Lender cleared the limit on purpose, so None, never warn. Any other value is the limit in paise. "Missing" and "empty" mean different things, which is why the comment explains it isn't stored like the other settings (chapter 010 covers the config table).

2. What was there before the change

  1. Payments are never edited (lesson 3), so any Payment the change writes has an id above this one.
  2. A Mortgage that didn't exist before (was is None), one that wasn't lent in cash before, or one now lent more. Lowering a cash Principal hands out no new cash, so it doesn't count.

3. The sums the change wrote in cash

  1. The heart of it: each part is added to its first part's total. A first part has received_with empty, so received_with or id is its own id; every other part names the first. That's trap #2's grouping, and the line the check-yourself exercise breaks from the other end.
  2. A list, not a total. Each sum is judged on its own: two different Payments of ₹15,000 aren't one sum of ₹30,000.

_preview (lending/previews.py, lines 157–164) calls before_change before running the change and cash_sums after, then rolls everything back. The New Mortgage Preview does the same (line 370). So every Preview answers with a cash list, whatever the change.

4. The warning, on the screen

A React hook that returns a function. Every form that proposes a change calls await warnCash(preview.previews?.cash ?? []) when the Lender presses Save (useProposedChange.tsx, line 56), and only saves if it comes back true. "Go back" is the default button. Chapter 011 covers hooks like this one; here, just notice that the comparison is at or above (largest < limit means "below, so go on"), and that a cleared limit (null) never asks.

(c) It only warns

Predict: the Lender sets the Cash Limit to ₹10,000, then something sends POST /api/payments for ₹25,000 in cash directly, with no Preview. What happens?

201: it's saved. Nothing on the save path reads the Cash Limit. The module says so in its first lines: "Only a warning: no Payment or Mortgage is refused for it, so nothing here is checked when saving." The app is a record of what happened. If Meera has already handed over the notes, refusing to record them would only make the book wrong. The Lender is warned, and decides.

Quiz

A Payment of ₹12,000 in cash goes to two Mortgages, ₹6,000 each, neither Redeemed. What is cash?

One payment row, so one entry: its amount, ₹12,000. Allocations aren't separate sums. And the backend lists every cash sum whatever the limit; the page compares.

Where is a ₹25,000 cash Payment checked against the Cash Limit?

The backend only reports what a change writes in cash, through the Preview. useCashWarning compares the largest sum with the setting and asks. The save itself is never refused.

The config table has no cash_limit row. What does GET /api/settings/cash-limit answer?

No row means never set, so DEFAULT_CASH_LIMIT. An empty row means the Lender cleared it: null.
