Chapter 007 · Lesson 5

Receipts

The Receipt is the one part of a Payment the Customer sees. This lesson rebuilds its figures from the Ledger, shows how it gathers a Payment's parts into one message, and explains why a Receipt resent months later still says the same thing.

By the end you can rebuild any Receipt's figures, and explain why it can be resent months later.

The problem

After Meera pays, she gets a WhatsApp message: what she paid, how each piece of jewellery's share was applied, and what each still owes. Three weeks later she calls: "I lost that message, can you send it again?"

The obvious design stores the message, or its figures, when the Payment is saved. But stored figures go stale (ADR 0001): if an earlier Payment turns up later, typed in from a paper book, the stored "still owed" is wrong. The other obvious design works it out from today's Ledger. But then the resent Receipt would show what Meera owes today, not what she owed just after that Payment, and wouldn't match the one on her phone.

There's a second constraint. The Lender's screens call the chain "2025-Meera-GoldChainPendant" and know it as Mortgage 2. Meera has never seen either. She knows it as her gold chain with the Lakshmi pendant.

The idea

Work the Receipt out from the replay every time it's asked for, and for each Mortgage read the Ledger as it stood just after that Payment on its date. The Ledger is replayed from facts, so asking again gives the same answer unless an earlier fact has changed, and then the Receipt is corrected with it. Name each Mortgage by its description, and the Customer by their name.

The flow

Notice the Receipt doesn't care which part you open it from. Lesson 2's Payment gives the same Receipt from 34 or from 35.

The code

1. What a line holds

There's no Mortgage Name and no id here: these are the fields a Customer's message is built from. receipts.py extends the line for the Lender's screen with mortgage_id (line 28: "for the Lender's links; never shown to the Customer"). @property makes total_owed read like a field but be worked out on each read. "Total owed" covers only the Mortgages this Payment paid, not everything the Customer owes.

2. Gathering the parts

  1. The same grouping as cash_sums in lesson 4: find the first part, whichever part you started from.
  2. A Voided part drops out. That's how lesson 2's Reopen left the Receipt with only the bangles.
  3. A list comprehension with two fors reads left to right like nested loops: for each part, for each of its Mortgages, a pair (part id, Mortgage row).
  4. The Receipt's amount is the sum of the parts: what the Customer handed over.

3. One line per Mortgage

  1. The split comes from the replay, as on the Payment page (lesson 1).
  2. The description, through descriptions() (nuance (d)).
  3. What the Mortgage owed just after this part: owed_after in replay.py, below. A Waiver's line is the exception (nuance (b)).
  4. "(Redeemed)" is added to the title when the Allocation was a Redemption's Final Payment.
  5. If a Mortgage appears in two parts, the second is folded into the first line. Today's record_payment never does that: each Mortgage is either ordinary or Redeemed. Releases before ADR 0011 could join a Final Payment to an earlier Payment on the same Mortgage ("Ready to Redeem"), and those Receipts still have to come out as one line.

4. "Just after the Payment"

  1. The Ledger up to the Payment's date, ending with that day's Interest to Date. Nothing after that date counts, so later Payments can't change it.
  2. Anything later the same day that lowered what's owed: another Payment, or a write-off.
  3. The Outstanding at the end of the day, with those added back. next(…) returns the first item of a generator: here, the index of this Payment's step.

Trap #16: customer-facing text hides internal names. Receipts and Notices use the jewellery's description and the Customer's name, never the Mortgage Name or ids. In load_receipt the Mortgage Name never reaches a ReceiptLine, and mortgage_id is only on ReceiptLineOut, for the Lender's links. test_the_receipt_message_shows_items_not_mortgage_names checks the text. When you add anything to a Receipt, Notice or message, keep to it (trap #16, owned by this lesson).

Nuances

(a) Resent later: the same, unless the past changed

Predict: after lesson 2, the chain is paid off and Redeemed. Open Receipt 23 again. Does the chain's line still say ₹81,253? And what if, instead, a ₹500 cash Payment to the chain dated 1 Sep were entered today?

From scripts/run_throughs.py (section L5), each on its own copy.

Lesson 2's Payment is after 10 Sep, so ledger(10 Sep) never sees it: the Receipt is unchanged. The 1 Sep Payment is before, so it changes what the chain owed on 10 Sep, and the Receipt changes with it, as it should. The "Paid" figures never change; only what was owed around them can. That's ADR 0001's promise: point-in-time history from facts that don't change.

owed_after gives ₹180: it adds back the day's later write-off (line 147), as if it hadn't happened yet. That's right for an ordinary Payment, but the Waiver's Final Payment came with the write-off. Telling Farida she "still owes ₹180" on the message that closes her Mortgage would be wrong. So load_receipt special-cases it (lines 90–94, 101–102): a Waiver's Final Payment line owes 0. It isn't marked "(Redeemed)": the jewellery was returned, but the Mortgage was Waived, not Redeemed.

(e) Two Payments on one day

On a copy, Meera pays the chain ₹2,000 by UPI (Payment 34) and then ₹1,000 in cash (Payment 35), both on 19 Oct. Receipt 34 says the chain still owes ₹81,333; Receipt 35 says ₹80,333 (from scripts/run_throughs.py, section L5). Each is "just after" its own Payment: owed_after adds 35's ₹1,000 back for Receipt 34, because it came later that day.

Quiz

Kavita's Receipt 26 is opened again next year. What will its "Still owed" say?

It's replayed up to 5 Oct, the Payment's date, every time. Later facts can't reach it. Closed Mortgages keep their Receipts; only a Voided Payment loses its own.

Which of these could appear in the text of Meera's Receipt?

A Receipt names each item by its description (trap #16). The Mortgage Name and ids are the Lender's labels; mortgage_id is in the JSON for the Lender's screen, never in the text.

Why does Farida's Receipt say ₹0 still owed, when owed_after gives ₹180?

load_receipt checks whether this Allocation was a Waiver's Final Payment and sets owed_after to 0: the ₹180 was let off with it. owed_after on its own adds the same-day write-off back.
