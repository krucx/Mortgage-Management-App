Chapter 007 · Lesson 1

A Payment and its Allocations

What exactly is stored when a Customer pays? This lesson reads one real Payment straight out of the database, then shows what the app refuses to store, and what it deliberately never stores at all.

By the end you can read the payment and allocation tables, say what PaymentIn and _refuse_payment refuse, and say why the interest/Principal split is never stored.

The problem

On 10 Sep 2026 Meera Sharma sends the Lender ₹12,000 by UPI. She has two Mortgages: a gold chain and a pair of bangles. She says "₹2,000 for the chain, ₹10,000 for the bangles". That's one sum of money, from one person, on one date, with one UPI reference. But it changes two Ledgers.

The obvious design is one row per Mortgage: "Meera paid ₹2,000 to the chain", "Meera paid ₹10,000 to the bangles". It falls apart quickly. The Lender's bank statement shows one ₹12,000, not two. The Receipt Meera gets should say "We have received ₹12,000". If the ₹12,000 was typed wrong, it should be corrected as one thing, not two that could drift apart.

There's a second question: should the row also say how much of the ₹2,000 paid interest and how much paid Principal? That's what the Lender wants to see. But that split depends on everything paid before it. Kavita's anklet shows why: its Payments were typed in from a paper book weeks late, in the wrong order.

The idea

Store the sum once, as a payment row, and store how it was divided between Mortgages as one Allocation row per Mortgage. Store nothing about what each Allocation paid off. That's worked out by replaying the Mortgage's Ledger every time someone asks, so it is always right, whatever was typed in later.

The flow

Notice what's missing: no interest, no Principal, no "still owed". Those come from the replay (trap #4, owned by chapter 004). Also notice amount is in paise, hundredths of a rupee, so ₹12,000 is stored as 1200000 (trap #6, owned by chapter 004).

The code

1. The tables

The tables are declared with SQLAlchemy Core: Table(…) describes a table in Python, and the code builds queries from it (select(payment_table)) instead of writing SQL strings. method is one of three values, from PaymentMethod in payments/payments.py (line 27): cash, upi, bank_transfer. METHOD_NAMES (line 34) turns them into "Cash", "UPI" and "Bank transfer" for the screens and Receipts.

2. What the request must look like: PaymentIn

  1. A Pydantic model describes the JSON body. FastAPI checks every request against it before your code runs, and answers 422 with Pydantic's own message if it doesn't fit: a missing field, an amount of 0 (gt=0 means "greater than 0"), no Allocations at all, or a method that isn't one of the three.
  2. A model_validator(mode="after") runs once every field has been checked on its own, so it can compare fields with each other. Raising ValueError here also becomes a 422.
  3. A blank or all-spaces reference is stored as NULL, not as an empty string.

3. What the database must allow: _refuse_payment

PaymentIn can only check the body on its own. Whether the Customer exists, or whether a Mortgage is theirs, needs the database. That's step 1 of every change (chapter 006: refuse → write → check → History):

  1. unprocessable (in platform/errors.py, chapter 006) raises a 422 with a message for the Lender.
  2. A Mortgage that doesn't exist and a Mortgage of someone else get the same message: either way, the Payment can't go there.
  3. A closed Mortgage (Redeemed, Waived or Forfeited) refuses with 409, "reopen it first" (_refuse_unless_active, line 176). 409 means "conflicts with the current state": the body is fine, the Mortgage just isn't open.

One refusal isn't here. A Payment larger than everything the Mortgage owed on its date is refused later, by check_replays after the write (chapter 006, lesson 2), because only a replay knows what was owed then.

4. The split, worked out on request

  1. mortgages_of (line 110) joins allocation to mortgage: each Mortgage the Payment went to, with its Allocation as allocated, in the order the Allocations were made.
  2. replays (chapter 005, lesson 1) replays each of those Mortgages from its facts.
  3. steps_by_payment (lending/replay.py, line 124) maps each Payment id to the Ledger step it made. That step knows its interest_paid and principal_paid, because the engine just applied it in date order.

Nuances

(a) Payments typed in the wrong order

Kavita's anklet was lent ₹15,000 in cash on 1 Jul 2026, at 2% a month. Its three Payments were typed in from a paper book on 18 Oct, and the last one typed carries the earliest date (it should have said 25 Sep; the Lender wrote 25 Aug by mistake). The ids show the typing order:

Predict: the anklet's 1 Aug interest was ₹300. Which Payment pays it, and how much Principal does Payment 31 (₹300 on 26 Aug) pay?

Replay orderPaymentDateInterestPrincipal

23126 Aug₹0₹300

From scripts/run_throughs.py (section L1): GET /api/payments/{31,32,33}.

The replay goes by date, not id. Payment 33, typed last, is applied first: it pays the ₹300 of interest and ₹14,700 of Principal. Payment 31 then finds no interest left and pays the last ₹300 of Principal. Payment 32 pays the 1 Sep interest, which was charged on the ₹15,000 the month started with (ADR 0006, trap #7). Had the split been stored when each Payment was typed, Payment 31 would say "₹300 interest" forever, and be wrong.

That's ADR 0001: anything that depends on earlier facts goes stale the moment an earlier fact changes, so it isn't stored. This includes the split.

Trap #1: where the writes live. This lesson's reads are in payments/payments.py, but PaymentIn, _refuse_payment and record_payment are in lending/changes.py. Every write that can move a Ledger lives there (trap #1, owned by chapter 003).

Quiz

Where does interest_paid on GET /api/payments/23 come from?

Neither table has it. load_payment replays each Mortgage and reads the step for this Payment from steps_by_payment(). Storing it would go stale whenever an earlier Payment is added or Voided, as the anklet shows.

Meera pays ₹1,000 to Kavita's anklet. What answers?

_refuse_payment compares each Mortgage's customer_id with the Payment's: "Each Mortgage must belong to the paying Customer". The anklet is Active, so 409 doesn't apply.

A body's Allocations add up to ₹1,000 and its amount says ₹2,000. Which code refuses it?

allocations_add_up is a Pydantic validator on PaymentIn. FastAPI runs it while parsing the body, so the route function never even starts.
