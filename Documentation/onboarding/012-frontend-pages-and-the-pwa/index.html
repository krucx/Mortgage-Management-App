Chapter 012

Frontend pages and the PWA

The problem this chapter solves

The Lender spends the whole day in about twenty screens. They look up a Customer, record a Payment, print a Receipt, close a Mortgage, phone the people on the Call List and send Notices on WhatsApp. Each screen has to be quick to read, and hard to get wrong. Chapter 011 showed how a screen asks for data. This chapter is about the screens themselves: how one is put together from the pieces already there, and how the app keeps opening on a laptop whose server may not be running.

Without a shared way of building pages, every new screen would choose its own loading message, its own error style, its own money format and its own confirmation. The Lender would learn twenty small dialects, and a developer would copy the wrong one. Without the installed app's service worker, the Lender who double-clicks the app's icon before starting the server gets a browser error page. And if the screens kept figures cached, they could show yesterday's Outstanding.

So the pages share one shape (ask, show "Loading…" or the error, then draw), one set of components for the hard parts (a Preview's before and after, a confirmation, History, photos), and one shell around them. The installed app caches the screens, never the figures.

What you'll be able to do

  • Find the file behind any screen from its URL, and read a page that loads and shows data.
  • Build a page from existing components, and wire a form to its Preview.
  • Follow how the screens divide a Payment and close a Mortgage.
  • Add a panel to the Mortgage page, and follow how photos and ID numbers are saved.
  • Change a follow-up screen or a setting, and say where each Dashboard figure comes from.
  • Say what the service worker caches and why, and what the Lender sees when the server is stopped or an update is out.

Before you start

This chapter builds on all of chapter 011, and on the business chapters it shows on screen:

  • The data layer. Chapter 011: the typed client, reading through one cache, useListPage, usePreview and useProposedChange, and what goes stale after a save. Every page here uses them; this chapter links back instead of teaching them again. The frontend sheet lists them.
  • Previews and changes. Chapter 006: a Preview runs the real change and rolls it back (trap #5), and every change writes History.
  • Closures. Chapter 005: Redeemed, Waived off, Forfeited, Dormant and Reopen, and why paying exactly the Outstanding is a Redemption (trap #3).
  • Payments, people and follow-up. 007 (Receipts and the Cash Limit), 008 (the Primary Number, ID Photos, Collateral Value) and 009 (the Call List, Notices and click-to-send).

Build the sample book fresh into a new, empty folder (chapter 011 saved a Payment and approved a Notice on the old one), then serve it on port 8000 and start the frontend:

# terminal 1, from backend/
export BOOK=$(mktemp -d)
uv run python ../docs/sample-book/book.py "$BOOK"
uv run python ../docs/sample-book/serve.py "$BOOK" 8000

# terminal 2, from frontend/
npm run dev

Open localhost:5173, with the browser's developer tools on the Network tab, filtered to /api/. Lessons 1–5 use this setup. Lesson 6 builds the screens and serves them on port 8765 instead, because the service worker only runs in a built app.

Two "todays". The served book's clock is held on 19 Oct 2026. Your browser's clock isn't. The screens take their default dates (and the latest date a date field accepts) from the browser. On the served book, a form therefore starts on your real date, which the backend refuses as a future date. On every dated form in this chapter, set the date to 19 Oct 2026 first. Lesson 1 shows why.

This chapter saves on your scratch book. In lesson 2 you add a Term Change to the bangles. In lesson 3 you Redeem Kavita's anklet. Lesson 4 replaces Meera's Aadhaar photo and adds a Customer, Deepa Nair. Lesson 5 adds a Call Note, sends Sunita's Notice, and saves the Notice template and puts it back. Lesson 6 starts again on a second fresh book. Rebuild before the next chapter.

The map

Notice that every page sits inside the same two wrappers. StartGate decides whether to show the app at all. The shell (header, menu, theme switch, update banner) is drawn once, around whichever page the URL picks. And every page reaches the backend only through chapter 011's data layer.
