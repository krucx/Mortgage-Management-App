Architecture review · pass 4

Mortgage-Management-App · 21 Oct 2026 · scope: does the SQLite database hold too much for a small Lender (about 100 Active Mortgages)?

Short answer: no. After a simulated 10 years with 100 Active Mortgages, the database is 5 MB (1 MB zipped) and the Dashboard loads in 83 ms. Storage won't be a problem. The only cost that grows with the age of the book is the Dashboard replaying every Mortgage, and that is still under 150 ms after 20 years.

Reading it

History is about 70% of the database, because it keeps one row per changed field: a Payment writes about 4 rows. That is many rows next to the data, but it is History's job (it is never edited or removed), and at about 270 KB a year it costs nothing worth saving.

Photos, not the database, will make Backups grow. They are kept as files and capped at 1600 px JPEG, so expect about 100–300 KB a photo, or tens of MB a year at worst. That is still fine for a cloud-synced folder (ADR-0003).

The Dashboard replays only the Mortgages that can still count

Speculative in-process · one query filter

Problem

To total "collected this year" and the month's Closures, the Dashboard replays every Mortgage ever made. Closed Mortgages pile up forever, so this is the only work that grows with the age of the book rather than its size.

Solution

Replay only Mortgages that are Active or were closed this year. Ones closed in earlier years can't add to either figure. This filters the query rather than storing a total, so ADR-0001 still holds.

Wins

  • Dashboard cost follows the ~100 live Mortgages
  • the interface is unchanged: test_dashboard.py carries over
  • one filter, nothing new to keep in sync
  • no stored totals that could go stale
Honest rating: 147 ms after 20 years is not a problem anyone will notice. Do it only if the book turns out much larger than 100 Mortgages.

Looked at, not worth it now

  • Trimming or archiving History. It goes against the glossary ("never edited or removed") and would save about 3 MB over 10 years.
  • Storing Ledger rows to speed up the Dashboard. It would reopen ADR-0001 to fix a speed problem that doesn't exist. The measurements back up that ADR's assumption that "replaying a few hundred Mortgages takes milliseconds" (ADR-0001 stands).
  • Moving off SQLite. One Lender on one laptop (ADR-0004) is exactly the job SQLite is built for, and 10 MB after 20 years is tiny for it.

Top recommendation

Leave the database as it is

At 100 Active Mortgages it grows about 0.5 MB a year, and the lists and Call List stay flat. If anything is ever done, make it 01, a single filter that caps the one cost that grows with time. Watch photo sizes in Backups more than the database.
