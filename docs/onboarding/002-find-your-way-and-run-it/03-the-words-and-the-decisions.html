Chapter 002 · Lesson 3

The words and the decisions

The code tells you what the app does. Two documents tell you what its words mean and why it does things the way it does. Where are they, how are they laid out, and how do you get from one to the other?

By the end you can find a glossary term, its Avoid words, and the ADR behind a rule.

The problem

You read in lending/ that Outstanding includes "today". A colleague's instinct says a figure owed should stop at yesterday, since today isn't over. Is the code wrong? Was it an accident, or a decision? If it was a decision, who made it, what else did they consider, and what would break if you "fixed" it?

Code can't answer that. Comments say what a line does, rarely what was rejected. Without a record, every newcomer re-argues the same questions, and some "fix" a rule that was right.

The idea

This repo keeps two short, plain records beside the code:

  • GLOSSARY.md at the root: every business word, its exact meaning, and the words not to use for it.
  • Documentation/adr/: Architecture Decision Records. Each ADR is one decision that a newcomer might otherwise question: what was decided, and why.

The glossary says what a word means; an ADR says why the rule behind it is that way. Together they are the first place to look before you change a rule.

The flow

The agent notes in Documentation/agents/domain.md tell every AI agent in this repo to follow the same path: read the glossary and the relevant ADRs before exploring, use the glossary's words, and flag it if a change contradicts an ADR. You're held to the same rules.

How the glossary is laid out

The glossary is grouped by section (People, Lending, Collateral, Payments, Follow-up, Safekeeping, Status), the same sections as the backend's packages from lesson 2. Each of its 50 terms has the same three-line shape.

  1. The term, in bold, with a capital letter. Wherever a capitalised word like Mortgage or Outstanding appears in code comments, docstrings, tests or issues, it means exactly this entry.
  2. One sentence of meaning. Other terms used inside it (Terms, Lender, Mortgage) are themselves entries.
  3. Words to avoid, because they mean something subtly different or invite confusion. Not every term has this line.

To find a term, search the file for **Default Terms**. The course's own glossary sheet copies the same entries word for word and says which lesson teaches each.

How an ADR is laid out

There are 12 ADRs, numbered 0001 to 0012. The file name is the number plus the decision in a few words, so the folder listing reads like a table of contents:

ADRDecision
0001The Ledger is derived, not stored
0002WhatsApp messages are click-to-send, not automated
0003Backups go to a cloud-synced folder, not email
0004The app ships as a Docker Compose stack on the Lender's laptop
0005A person is identified by their Primary Number
0006Each month's interest is charged on the Principal at the start of that month
0007Each change's Preview route is written out by hand beside its save route
0008The installed PWA starts and updates the app through a mortgage-management:// link
0009Each version is installed in a folder of its own, and app is a junction to the one that runs
0010Without Docker, the backend serves the app's built screens itself
0011Outstanding includes the Interest to Date, today included
0012Dormant Mortgages are flagged, not Redeemed automatically

Inside, an ADR is short: a heading that states the decision, then one to three paragraphs giving the situation, the decision and its reasons. Some end with Considered Options, the alternatives and why they lost. A new decision takes the next number. When a decision replaces an older rule, its ADR says what it replaced and why, as 0011 does about the old Redemption Amount.

Rule: if your change contradicts an ADR, say so in the issue or commit and argue for reopening it. Never quietly work around one.

Nuances

Predict: you're naming a new function that returns the total the Customer must pay to get their jewellery back today. Which name fits this repo: redemption_amount, balance_due or outstanding?

outstanding. Both others are on Outstanding's Avoid list: "Redemption Amount" is a retired term from before ADR 0011, and "balance" suggests a stored running total, which this app deliberately doesn't keep (ADR 0001). The same goes for screen labels, test names and issue titles.

Predict: you need a word for "a Mortgage the Customer has stopped paying, that the Lender is about to sell". It isn't Forfeited yet. What do you do?

Don't invent a synonym like "defaulted" (it's on Forfeited's Avoid list) or "overdue". The glossary has no term for this, which is a signal: either the idea isn't needed, or there's a gap worth raising with the owner. domain.md says the same: a missing term is something to note, not something to coin quietly.

Quiz

Where do you find why each Preview route is written out by hand rather than generated?

It's a design decision, so it's an ADR: 0007, "Each change's Preview route is written out by hand beside its save route". The glossary says what a Preview is, not how its routes are built. A docstring might mention it, but the ADR is the record.

An issue says "the borrower's balance is wrong". How should it be worded?

Customer and Outstanding are the glossary's terms. Client and loanee are on Customer's Avoid list; dues and payoff are on Outstanding's.

You disagree with ADR 0012 and think Dormant Mortgages should Redeem by themselves. What's the right move?

An ADR records a decision someone made for reasons. Contradicting one is fine, but out loud: cite it and argue. Tests check behaviour, not reasons, and deleting the ADR would throw away the reasons it records.
