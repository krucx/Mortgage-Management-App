Chapter 004 · Lesson 1

The Ledger is a replay

On 18 Oct the Lender typed Kavita's anklet in from the paper book, with three Payments. The last one typed was dated before the other two. It changed how the other two were applied, though nobody edited them. This lesson shows the function that makes that happen, and what it's given.

By the end you can say what is stored, what is derived, and why a back-dated Payment rewrites later Ledger steps that nobody edited.

The problem

Kavita pledged a silver-gold anklet on 1 Jul 2026 for ₹15,000 in cash. Its Terms are 2% a month, Interest Mode Simple, and Part-Month Rule "nothing". The Lender only got round to entering it on 18 Oct, and typed its Payments in the order the slips came to hand:

TypedPayment idDatedAmount
1st3126 Aug 2026₹300
2nd325 Sep 2026₹300
3rd3325 Aug 2026₹15,000

Picture a design that stores the Ledger as rows, the way the app's first spec did (a MortgageLedger table). After the first two Payments, the rows say Payment 31 paid ₹300 of interest. Then Payment 33 arrives, dated a day earlier. It pays most of the Principal, so now Payment 31 should have paid Principal, not interest. September's and October's rows are wrong too. The stored design needs code that finds every row after 25 Aug and rewrites it correctly. Every other kind of change (a Void, a corrected date, a Term Change) needs that code as well. If any of it has a bug, the figures drift and nobody notices.

The idea

Store only facts that never change: the Mortgage's start date, amount and Terms, its Term Changes, and each Payment's Allocation with its date. Whenever anyone needs the Ledger, Replay the facts from the start date, in date order, and rebuild it. There's nothing to rewrite, because nothing derived was kept (ADR 0001).

Think of a chess game written down as its list of moves. You don't store the board after every move. You store the moves, and play them out whenever you want to see the board. If you find a move was written down wrong, you fix that one line and play the game through again.

The flow

Notice that the arrow out of compute_ledger goes nowhere near the database. The list of steps is used for one answer and then thrown away.

The code

1. What goes in: an Allocation

A Payment enters the engine as just three things: when, how much, and which Payment it belongs to. @dataclass(frozen=True) makes a small class whose fields can't be changed after it's made. Amounts are always paise, integers (₹1 = 100 paise), so no floating-point rounding ever creeps in. Lesson 2 has more on units.

2. What comes out: a LedgerStep

  1. Every step carries the running Principal and unpaid interest after it, so the last step tells you where the Mortgage stands.
  2. How a Payment divided between interest and Principal lives here, on a derived step, and not on the allocation row. That's the "₹1,500 to interest, ₹3,500 to Principal" from chapter 003.

3. The replay: compute_ledger

  1. as_of is the day to replay up to, included. Ask for 19 Oct and you get the Ledger as it stands at the end of 19 Oct.
  2. _Run holds the running state of one replay: the Principal, the unpaid interest, and the Payments not yet applied, sorted by date. A fresh _Run is made for every call. Nothing carries over between calls.
  3. accrue_through walks from the start to as_of, one Accrual Date at a time, applying the Payments that fall before and on each one. Lessons 2–4 open it up.
  4. An open Ledger ends with today's Interest to Date: the interest for the month under way so far (lessons 2 and 3).

The parameters marked "chapter 005" are how the same function closes a Mortgage. In this chapter they're always left at None. The only caller in the app is MortgageReplay._replay, at lending/replay.py line 265, which reads the stored facts and passes them in. Chapter 005 opens it.

This is where Kavita's late slip finds its place. The Allocations come in "in no particular order" (as replay.py says), and they're sorted by date, then by Payment id for two on the same day. The order they were typed in doesn't matter.

Payment 31 was typed first and never touched again. Yet its step went from "₹300 to interest" to "₹300 to Principal". That's because the replay now meets Payment 33 first, which clears August's interest and all but ₹300 of the Principal. October's interest dropped to nothing, because there's no Principal left to charge.

One figure looks odd: on 1 Sep, ₹300 of interest is added though the Principal is already ₹0. That isn't a bug. September's month began on 1 Aug, when the Principal was ₹15,000, and a month is charged on its starting Principal. Lesson 3 explains why (ADR 0006). Payment 32 pays that interest, and the anklet owes nothing. The Lender still holds it, so it's flagged Dormant (chapter 005).

Check it on your served book: open /mortgages/10 (2026-Kavita-SilverGoldAnklet) and click Show complete Ledger. The steps match the last block above. The 1 Sep line reads "Interest: 2% of ₹15,000": that's charged_on, the Principal at the month's start. The 1 Oct line reads "2% of ₹0" and moves ₹0.

Nuances

(a) Always from the start

Some systems that allow back-dated facts look for the "branch point": the first event affected by the late fact. They undo the events after it and replay only from there. Martin Fowler calls this pattern a Retroactive Event. This app never does that. Every call to compute_ledger starts again at "started". A Mortgage has at most a few dozen steps, so a few hundred Mortgages replay in milliseconds (ADR 0001). The simple design is also the one that can't drift.

(b) A history that can't have happened

Predict: on the anklet's served book, the Lender tries to add a fourth Payment of just ₹1, dated 25 Aug. On 25 Aug it owed far more than ₹1. Is it accepted?

No. The Preview answers 422:

2026-Kavita-SilverGoldAnklet: the Payment on 26 Aug 2026 would be more than the ₹299 owed then

From scripts/run_throughs.py (section L1), POST /api/payments/preview on the built book.

The ₹1 itself is fine. But it comes first in the replay, so by 26 Aug only ₹299 is owed, and Payment 31's ₹300 is now more than that. pay_through raises Overpayment for the later Payment (lesson 3 shows the check). A back-dated fact is refused when it would make any later fact impossible, and the message names the Payment that broke.

Trap #5, a warning: the change is refused before it saves, and its Preview is refused too, because a Preview runs the real change and rolls it back. Chapter 006 owns how. See the traps sheet.

(c) Two Payments on the same day

Same-day Allocations are applied in Payment-id order (line 188): the one saved first goes first. So it's still deterministic: the same facts always give the same Ledger.

Newcomer trap #4: nothing owed is stored. There's no ledger table, no stored Outstanding, and no stored "interest paid". A slow page is never a reason to add one. Cache inside a request if you must (MortgageReplay._ledgers keeps each Ledger for the life of one request), but never across requests. This chapter owns trap #4; see the traps sheet.

Quiz

Which of these is a stored fact, not derived?

The Allocation's amount is in the allocation row. How it divided (₹300 + ₹14,700) and every interest step come out of compute_ledger each time.

A Payment from June is Voided. What has to update every later Ledger step?

There are no stored steps to rewrite. replays() loads only non-Voided Allocations, so the next call to compute_ledger simply never sees it.

Two Payments to one Mortgage share a date. Which is applied first?

_Run sorts by (a.on, a.payment_id). Ids grow as Payments are saved, so the earlier-saved one goes first. Amount plays no part.
