Chapter 006

Changes and Previews

The problem this chapter solves

The Lender changes the book many times a day. They record a Payment, correct a Principal they typed wrong, move the bangles to 1.5% a month, Void a ₹16,000 that should have been ₹6,000, close a Mortgage, and Reopen one closed by mistake. Every one of these changes raises the same four questions. Is it allowed? What does it write? Does every Ledger it touches still make sense afterwards? And who changed what, and when?

There's a fifth question, too: what would it do? The Lender sees a Preview of every change before saving it. If the Preview had its own copy of the rules, sooner or later the two copies would drift apart. The Lender would see a Preview of a change that then fails to save, or worse, a Preview with different figures from the ones that get saved. If each save route checked things in its own way, one of them would forget to record History, or forget to check that a correction doesn't leave an old Payment larger than what was owed.

This app answers all five questions in one place. Every change to a Mortgage or Payment is one function, in the package that owns its concept (payments/, lending/ or collateral/), and every one follows the same four steps: refuse → write → check → History. A Preview doesn't copy any of it. It runs that same function and then rolls the database back.

What you'll be able to do

  • Name every change, find its save route and its Preview route, and walk one through refuse → write → check → History.
  • Say why the check comes after the write, and which changes it can refuse.
  • Read a Mortgage's History and say what each kind of change records.
  • Say exactly what a Preview runs, and why it is refused exactly when saving would be.
  • List everything a new change needs: the change function, the save route, the Preview route, a History Action, and tests.

Before you start

Chapter 003, lesson 3 saved one Payment and showed you record_payment once. Here you meet the pattern that every change follows. Chapter 004 taught that nothing owed is stored: every figure is replayed from facts. That idea is why the check in lesson 2 works the way it does. Chapter 005 taught the Closure rules. This chapter shows the routes that apply them.

Keep the sample book served from chapter 002, lesson 4 (http://127.0.0.1:8765, held on 19 Oct 2026). Build it fresh into a new empty folder and set $BOOK to that folder. Chapter 003's Payment and chapter 005's Reopens change the figures and refusals quoted here. In this chapter you'll try changes that are refused, and ask for Previews. Neither saves anything. Never try them against real data.

Lessons

  1. One door for every changeThe four steps, the bangles' Term Change, and the two refusals you can provoke: 422 and 409.
  2. The check: replay, then refuseCorrect starting Terms or lower Meera's chain to ₹1,000, then watch the replay and Preview explain what changed or refuse an impossible Payment.
  3. HistoryWhat each change records: the chain's description edit, the Voided ₹16,000, and a Term Change that stores only the rate.
  4. Previews: make it, then roll it backRemove the bangles' coming Term Change, then pay off the chain, both without saving anything.
  5. Adding a change routeCollateral Value, the smallest complete example, as a checklist. Then sketch a change of your own on paper.
  6. Check yourselfWhich step refuses it? 409 or 422? Then delete one line from edit_mortgage and see the refusal, the History and the Preview all fail together.
