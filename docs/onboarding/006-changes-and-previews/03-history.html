Chapter 006 · Lesson 3

History

The last step of every change is audit.record, which adds rows to the Mortgage's or Payment's History. This lesson reads the sample book's History and shows what each kind of change leaves behind.

By the end you can read a Mortgage's History and say what each change records.

The problem

On 10 Oct the Lender typed ₹16,000 for one of Meera's Payments instead of ₹6,000, Voided it, and entered ₹6,000. Months later Meera asks why her bangles' Ledger once showed a ₹16,000 Payment. Or the Lender wonders who changed the chain's description, and when. The Ledger can't answer: it is replayed from today's facts, and a Voided Payment isn't in it at all. Something has to remember what the facts were, and how they changed.

The glossary calls this History: the record of every change to a Customer, Mortgage or Payment, with when, what, and the old and new values, which is never edited or removed.

Say "History". The code calls the module audit.py, the table audit_entry, and the module's docstring still says "The audit log". In this course, in the app and in new code, it's History. The glossary lists Log under Avoid.

The idea

History is an append-only table with one row per changed field. Each row says when (at), about what (entity and entity_id), which kind of change (action), the field, its old and new values as text, and an optional note. A change that has no fields to show, such as a Redemption, gets one row with only an action and a note.

Nothing writes History automatically: there are no database triggers. Each change function calls audit.record itself, as its last step, inside the same transaction as the change. So a refused change leaves no History, and a saved one always does.

The flow

Notice there's no update or delete arrow into audit_entry. The only code that writes to it is the insert in record.

The code

1. The kinds of entry

  1. Literal[…] is a type that allows only these exact strings. mypy refuses audit.record(…, "renamed") because "renamed" isn't in the list, and the generated API types give the frontend the same list. A new kind of entry starts here (lesson 5).
  2. One field's old and new values, both as text, or None where there was no value.

2. Working out what changed

  1. _text (lines 47–52) turns a date into 2026-10-25, anything else into str(value), and keeps None. Values are compared as text, so ₹80,000 is stored as 8000000: paise, as the code holds it.
  2. dict.fromkeys(fields) is a dict with every field set to None. So "created" is just "changed from nothing", and a field created empty gives no row at all.

The caller chooses the fields. edit_mortgage passes _AUDITED_EDITS (changes.py lines 164–173: name, start date, Principal, description, weights, purity, Lent Through) and create_mortgage passes _AUDITED_ON_CREATE (lines 473–486: those, plus the Customer and the three Terms). Fields not on the list, like status or the Closure columns, are recorded by their own actions instead.

3. Writing it

  1. Every row of one change gets the same at, action and note, so a reader can group them back into one change.
  2. a or b gives b when a is an empty list. No changed fields means one row with only the action and note.

4. Reading it back

  1. The Mortgage Name comes from a join with today's mortgage row, not from the entry. So every entry shows the Mortgage's current name (nuance b).
  2. Ordered by id, which grows with every insert, so newest first even when two entries share a timestamp.
  3. A Mortgage's History is its own entries plus those of every Payment with an Allocation to it, Voided ones included. customer_history (line 147) does the same one level up: the Customer's entries, plus their Mortgages' and Payments'.

What to notice:

  • The chain's edit recorded one row, for the one field that changed. The other seven fields in _AUDITED_EDITS were sent too, unchanged, and recorded nothing.
  • The ₹16,000 Payment was created with three rows, not four: its reference was empty, and a field created empty gives no row. Its Void added a row for void_reason. Both are payment entries, yet they appear in the bangles' History because Payment 27 had an Allocation to the bangles. The ₹16,000 isn't in the Ledger any more, but it's still in History.
  • The Term Change recorded the date and the one Term that differs. Interest Mode and Part-Month Rule stayed the same, so, as in its term_change row (lesson 1), they aren't mentioned.

The bangles' whole History is 25 rows: 12 for being created, 4 for the 10 Sep Payment, 7 for the two 10 Oct Payments and the Void, and 2 for the Term Change. Meera's Customer History has 73: her own entries as a Customer, plus those of her two Mortgages and of her Payments.

Notice the two places a reason can go. A Void stores its reason as the new value of void_reason, the same column it sets on the payment row. A Reopen stores the status change as its field, and its reason in note.

Every entry shows the new name, because mortgage_name is joined from today's mortgage row. The old name isn't lost: it's in the values of the name rows. That's the History being what the glossary says: never edited. Only the label beside it is current, so the Lender can find the Mortgage. Payment entries have no mortgage_name at all (None), since one Payment can go to several Mortgages.

(d) Nothing changed, nothing recorded; refused, nothing recorded

Save the chain's Edit form without changing anything and the History stays at the same number of rows: changed is empty, and line 469 of edit_mortgage skips record. A refused change records nothing either, as lesson 2's ₹1,000 edit showed (271 rows before and after). That second rule is pinned by test_a_refused_change_records_no_history in tests/test_changes.py.

History is mostly tested inside the tests of each change: test_an_edit_is_written_and_recorded, test_term_changes_are_in_the_history, test_closing_and_reopening_are_in_the_history and others assert the entries a change leaves. tests/test_audit.py itself is tiny: three tests of changes().

Quiz

The Lender edits the chain, changing its net weight and its purity. How many History rows does that add?

record writes one row per Change, and audit.changes lists only fields whose text differs. Both rows share the same at and action.

Where in History is the reason for Voiding the ₹16,000 Payment?

_record_void_history records Change("void_reason", None, reason) with no note. It's the Reopen that puts its reason in note.

Why is the Voided ₹16,000 Payment in the bangles' History?

mortgage_history takes the Mortgage's entries plus the entries of every Payment allocated to it, Voided or not. The Void itself is one payment entry.
