Chapter 009 · Lesson 3

The Notice queue is synced, not scheduled

On 19 Oct there is a Notice waiting for Sunita, about interest that will be added on 21 Oct. Nobody created it by hand, and no timer fired. This lesson shows what made it, and when the queue is worked out again.

By the end you can say which unsent Notices should exist today, and when they are worked out again.

The problem

A Notice tells a Customer, a day or two ahead, that interest is about to be added: "On 21 Oct, interest of ₹700 will be added." The Lender wants these ready each morning, without remembering anyone's Accrual Date.

The usual answer is a scheduler: a job that runs at 6 a.m. and creates the day's Notices. This app has none. It runs on the Lender's laptop, which may be shut all weekend, or asleep at 6 a.m. (trap #8). A job that only runs at a set time would miss those days, and nobody would notice.

Settings change too. If the Lender switches from "2 days ahead" to "5 days ahead", Notices should appear for the next 5 days at once, not tomorrow morning. And a Mortgage that is Redeemed today shouldn't still get a Notice on Thursday.

The idea

One function, sync, makes the unsent Notices what they should be today, under the current settings. It doesn't add "today's batch". It compares the queue with what ought to exist and fixes the difference. So it can be run as often as you like: running it twice changes nothing. In the code's word, it's a sync, not a job.

Because it's safe to repeat, the app runs it whenever the queue might be looked at: on the first /api/ request of each day, on every read of the Notices page or the Dashboard, and whenever the message settings are saved.

Think of a thermostat rather than an alarm clock. An alarm goes off at 6 a.m. and does something once. A thermostat checks the room whenever it looks and nudges it towards the setting. Looking twice does no harm.

The flow

Notice what a pair is: a Customer and a date, not a Mortgage. If two of a Customer's Mortgages accrue on the same day, they share one Notice.

The code

1. The statuses

A StrEnum is an enum whose members are also plain strings, so NoticeStatus.SENT == "sent" and the database stores the text. sync only ever touches the two unsent statuses. A sent or skipped Notice is history: sync never changes or removes it. Lesson 4 follows a Notice through all four.

2. Which dates are coming

  1. The month count from the start to after: a good first guess at which Accrual Date comes next. The first Accrual Date is one month after the start, so n is at least 1.
  2. := is Python's "walrus" operator: it assigns and tests in one go. accrual_date(start, n) (chapter 004) is the n-th Accrual Date, clamped to month-end when the month is shorter.
  3. Strictly after today. A Notice is advance warning, so an Accrual Date today, or already past, never gets one.

3. sync, step by step

Step 1 withdraws unsent Notices that are now too far ahead. That only happens after the Lender lowers the days ahead. Deleting them loses nothing: an unsent Notice is only a Customer and a date, so it will be made again when its date comes within range.

  1. A set of (customer_id, date) tuples, so two Mortgages of one Customer on the same day collapse into one entry. The table backs this up with UniqueConstraint("customer_id", "accrues_on") in platform/db.py.
  2. Dormant is checked on the Accrual Date, from the replay. A Mortgage that owes nothing then has nothing to warn about.

Line 137 is the heart of it: accruing - already is set difference, "what should exist, minus what does". On a second run that difference is empty, which is why syncing twice changes nothing. already includes sent and skipped Notices too, so a Notice the Lender skipped is never made again.

Step 4 decides each unsent Notice's status from scratch. With "approve first" on, a Notice is held (awaiting_approval) until the Lender has approved it, and queued after. With it off, nothing is held, and line 151 forgets every approval. not_ and false() are SQLAlchemy's way of writing SQL NOT and FALSE.

4. Who calls sync

  1. DailyRun runs its jobs once per business day, on the first /api/ request of that day, from middleware. How it does that is chapter 010, lesson 2's; here it's enough that sync_notices is one of its two jobs.
  2. The Dashboard counts Notices to send and to approve. Syncing first means those counts match what the Notices page will show.
  3. So a Mortgage added this morning, or one just Reopened, gets its Notice the moment anyone looks.
  4. after_set is a list of functions ConfigGroup.set calls after saving, on the same connection. The import sits inside the function because notices.py imports this module: importing it at the top would make a circle.
These GETs write. GET /api/notices and GET /api/dashboard open engine.begin(), not engine.connect(), because the sync inside them can insert, delete and update notice rows. If a page "changed the database" when you only loaded it, this is why.

Turn "approve first" off, then on

This saves a setting on your served book, then puts it back. PUT takes the whole settings object, so read it, change one field, and send it back (jq does the edit):

S=$(curl -s http://127.0.0.1:8765/api/settings/messages)
printf '%s' "$S" | jq '.notice_approve_first = false' | curl -s -X PUT -H 'content-type: application/json' --data-binary @- http://127.0.0.1:8765/api/settings/messages
printf '%s' "$S" | jq '.notice_approve_first = true'  | curl -s -X PUT -H 'content-type: application/json' --data-binary @- http://127.0.0.1:8765/api/settings/messages
Keep the templates' line breaks. The settings include both templates, whose JSON holds escapes. In zsh, the macOS default shell, echo "$S" turns those escapes into real newlines, and curl -d @- then strips real newlines from what it sends. The PUT still succeeds, and every template is saved run together on one line. Use printf '%s' and --data-binary @-, which pass the bytes through unchanged. (Found while checking this lesson.)

Saving other settings keeps an approval (test_saving_settings_with_approval_on_keeps_approved_notices_approved). Turning approval off wipes it (line 151). So turning it back on holds every unsent Notice, even ones approved before. The reasoning: if the Lender turns checking back on, they want to check everything that hasn't gone out yet.

The missed days are made up, but only for Accrual Dates still to come (on > after in _coming_accrual_dates). A warning about the 25th is useless on the 26th, so it is never made. Sunita's Notice was already made, so it stays, awaiting approval, after its date. sync doesn't tidy old unsent Notices; the Lender skips them (lesson 4).

(d) A Dormant Mortgage gets no Notice

Kavita's anklet accrues on 1 Nov. With days ahead set to 14 on 19 Oct (until 2 Nov), the queue gains Meera's 25 Oct Notice, but nothing for Kavita: the anklet is Dormant on 1 Nov, so line 127 drops it (ADR 0012). (From scripts/run_throughs.py, section L3 (e).)

(e) One per Customer per date

From July to October 2026, the 10th has two sent Notices each month. They aren't duplicates: they're two Customers, Meera (her chain) and Imran (his earrings, until they were Forfeited). If Meera had a second Mortgage accruing on the 10th, she'd still get one Notice, covering both. Lesson 5 shows such a Notice.

Quiz

On 19 Oct the Lender lowers days ahead from 2 to 1. What happens to Sunita's 21 Oct Notice?

until becomes 20 Oct, so step 1 deletes the unsent Notice for 21 Oct (from scripts/run_throughs.py, section 99). On 20 Oct, 21 Oct is within 1 day and the first request of the day makes it again. Only sent or skipped Notices are kept as they are.

Which of these does not run sync?

Payments don't sync. They don't need to: the next read of the Notices or the Dashboard syncs, and an unsent Notice's figures are worked out on read anyway. The daily run, both reads and the message settings' after_set are the four callers.

With "approve first" on, the Lender skips a Notice for 25 Oct. The page is reloaded. Is a new Notice made for that Customer and day?

already is every Notice dated after today, whatever its status. Skipping is a decision, and sync respects it.
