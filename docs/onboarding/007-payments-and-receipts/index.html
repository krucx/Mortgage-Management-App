Chapter 007

Payments and Receipts

The problem this chapter solves

Money comes in to the Lender every day. Meera hands over ₹12,000 by UPI and says "₹2,000 for the chain, the rest for the bangles". Lakshmi brings ₹2,03,600 by bank transfer to take her necklace home. Someone types ₹16,000 when they meant ₹6,000. Kavita brings ₹25,000 in notes, and the Lender has to remember that Government of India rules want sums that large paid by UPI or bank transfer. And every Customer expects a message on WhatsApp saying what they paid and what they still owe.

Each of those needs care. A Payment that covers two Mortgages has to be divided between them, in Allocations that add up. A mistake can't just be deleted or edited, because the Ledger of every Mortgage it touched would quietly change, with no trace of why. The cash warning has to treat "one sum" the way the rules do, even when the app stores that sum as two rows. And the Receipt has to show the same figures if the Lender resends it next month, and must never show the Customer the Lender's internal labels.

Each Mortgage may also receive a Discount: a non-cash reduction beside its cash Allocation. Discounts affect the replay and Receipt, but not cash received, cash warnings, or cash-only reports.

This chapter is about how the app stores cash Allocations and non-cash Discounts on a Payment, how it corrects one, when it warns about cash, and how it works out a Receipt.

What you'll be able to do

  • Read the payment and allocation tables, and say what PaymentIn and _refuse_payment refuse.
  • Say how many payment rows a Payment makes, which id comes back, and why the API never shows received_with.
  • Correct a wrong Payment with a Void and a new Payment, and say which Voids are refused.
  • Say what counts as one cash sum, and why the Cash Limit only warns.
  • Explain how the Payment Preview decides a Discount's room and threshold, how it is applied after cash, and how it is reversed with its Payment; distinguish it from cash-based reports.
  • Rebuild any Receipt's figures, and explain why it can be resent months later.

Before you start

Chapter 003 followed one Payment from the form to the Receipt. This chapter opens each step it passed through. Chapter 004, lesson 3 taught what a Payment pays first inside one Mortgage. Chapter 005, lesson 3 taught that paying exactly the Outstanding Redeems a Mortgage. Chapter 006 taught the four steps every change follows (refuse → write → check → History) and how a Preview runs the real change and rolls it back. This chapter doesn't re-teach any of these. It uses them.

Keep the sample book served from chapter 002, lesson 4 (http://127.0.0.1:8765, held on 19 Oct 2026). Build it fresh into a new empty folder and set $BOOK to that folder: chapter 003's Payment and chapter 005's Reopens change the ids and figures quoted here. In lesson 2 you'll save one Payment on it, and the later lessons expect that Payment to be there. Everything else you try is a Preview, a read, or a change that gets refused. Never try any of it against real data.

You'll also read the database directly with sqlite3, which ships with macOS and most Linux systems. The sample book's database is $BOOK/data/mortgage.db. Only read it; every write goes through the API.

The map

Notice that nothing flows from the tables to the screens without a replay in between. The tables hold only what was paid; how it was applied, and what is still owed, is worked out each time it is asked for.
