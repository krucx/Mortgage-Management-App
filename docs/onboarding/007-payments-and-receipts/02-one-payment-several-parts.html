Chapter 007 · Lesson 2

One Payment, several parts

In lesson 1, one Payment was one payment row. That isn't always so. When a Payment Redeems a Mortgage, the app stores it as two or more rows that belong together. This lesson shows when that happens, which id you get back, and why the API never tells you.

By the end you can explain received_with: when a Payment is split into parts, which id comes back, and why the API hides it.

The problem

It's 19 Oct. Meera comes in to take her chain home and to pay something towards the bangles. She hands over one UPI transfer: ₹83,333 for the chain (exactly its Outstanding today) and ₹1,000 for the bangles. Paying exactly the Outstanding Redeems the chain in the same request (trap #3, owned by chapter 005).

So the ₹83,333 is the chain's Final Payment. Now picture two things that could happen next:

  • The chain was handed back by mistake. The Lender Reopens it. The Reopen must Void the chain's Final Payment (chapter 005, lesson 5). But a Void cancels a whole payment row. If the ₹83,333 and the ₹1,000 were one row, Reopening the chain would also wipe out the ₹1,000 Meera really did pay on the bangles.
  • Meera wants her Receipt. She paid ₹84,333 in one transfer, so she expects one Receipt that says ₹84,333.

The first wants two rows. The second wants one Payment.

The idea

Store each Final Payment as a part of its own: a separate payment row with its own Allocation. Link every part to the Payment's first part through the received_with column. The ordinary Allocations together are the first part; if every Allocation Redeems, the first Final Payment is. Anything that thinks of "the Payment" (the Receipt, the cash warning) gathers the parts back together through received_with. Anything that acts on one Mortgage's Final Payment (a Reopen) touches only its part.

The flow

Notice the order doesn't depend on the body. Whether the chain or the bangles comes first in the request, the ordinary part is written first.

The code

1. Sorting the Allocations

  1. _redeemed_by (line 325) returns the replayed Mortgage if the Allocation equals its whole Outstanding on the Payment date, and None otherwise. If the Mortgage can't be Redeemed on that date, it refuses the whole Payment (chapter 005, lesson 3).
  2. model_copy(update=…) makes a copy of a Pydantic model with some fields replaced. Here it's the same Payment with only the ordinary Allocations, and an amount that adds up to them.
  3. The first part is written. Its id becomes first.

2. Each Final Payment, as a part of its own

  1. The same _close_redeemed that the Redeem route uses (chapter 005), told which Payment this part belongs to. Line 316 is the line the check-yourself exercise breaks.
  2. If there was no ordinary part, the first Final Payment written becomes the first part, and later ones point at it.
  3. The id returned is always the first part's.

3. Where received_with is written

model_dump() turns the Pydantic model into a dict; ** spreads it into keyword arguments. .returning(…) asks the database for the new row's id in the same statement. The second insert is given a list of dicts, so it writes one allocation row per Allocation.

_close_redeemed reaches this through _close_with_payment (line 600), which builds a one-Allocation PaymentIn for the Final Payment, calls _write_payment(conn, final, received_with) at line 630, sets the Closure, and only then runs check_replays. The comment at lines 616–617 says why it's checked after closing: the Final Payment may include the Interest to Date, which is owed only once the Mortgage closes.

Trap #2: one Payment can be several payment rows. A Final Payment is kept as a part of its own, pointing back through received_with, which the API never shows; the id POST /api/payments returns is the first part. When you count Payments, sum cash, or build anything "per Payment", group the rows by received_with or id, the way load_receipt and cash_sums do (trap #2, owned by this lesson).

Nuances

(a) Every Allocation Redeems

From scripts/run_throughs.py (section L2).

There's no ordinary part, so first is still None when the loop starts. The chain's Final Payment is written with received_with=None and becomes the first part (line 320). The bangles' points at it. The route returns 34. Both Mortgages are Redeemed, and the Receipt is ₹2,34,833 with nothing still owed. With just one Allocation that Redeems (the chain alone), there's a single row and received_with stays empty: a Payment of one part is an ordinary-looking row.

(b) A Reopen takes only its own part

Back on the Payment you saved. Suppose the chain was handed back by mistake and the Lender Reopens it. Don't do this on your served book (the next lessons expect the chain Redeemed); here's what scripts/run_throughs.py saw on a copy:

After POST /mortgages/2/reopenResult

Receipt of 34₹1,000: only the bangles' line

That's the glossary's Final Payment in action: "kept as its own part of the Payment it came in, so a Reopen Voids it alone and leaves the rest". The Reopen Voids redemption_payment_id, which is 35, through void_payment (chapter 006, lesson 2).

(c) Why the API hides received_with

Look at Payment in payments/payments.py (line 55): no received_with. And a test makes sure it never appears anywhere in the API:

The history explains it. Before ADR 0011, a Payment could leave a Mortgage "Ready to Redeem", and a later Redeem request carried received_with to join its Final Payment to that earlier Payment's Receipt. A client could name any Payment there. Once paying the whole Outstanding Redeemed the Mortgage in the same request, that flow was retired (commit 2743eac), and received_with left the API. Now only record_payment sets it, inside one transaction, for parts it has just written itself. Nothing outside the backend needs it either: the Receipt and the cash sums gather the parts on the server, and the id you get back is enough to find them.

The test reads the generated OpenAPI schema (schema_text()), so it fails the moment someone adds received_with to any request or response model.

(d) A whole Outstanding that can't Redeem

If an Allocation equals a Mortgage's whole Outstanding but the Mortgage can't be Redeemed on that date (say a later Payment exists), _redeemed_by refuses, and the whole Payment is refused with it, ordinary part included: all or none. Chapter 005, lesson 3 owns that rule; test_if_the_redemption_is_refused_the_ordinary_part_is_not_recorded_either pins it.

Quiz

Arjun pays ₹1,000 on his ring and his chain's whole Outstanding, chain listed first. Which part is received_with empty?

The ordinary Allocations are always written first, whatever the body's order, and become the first part. Each Final Payment points at it. There is no separate "Payment" row for both to point at.

Why is a Final Payment stored as a part of its own?

A Void cancels a whole payment row. Keeping the Final Payment apart lets a Reopen Void it and leave the rest of the Payment standing. It shares the Payment's reference, and the Receipt merges the parts back into one.

After the run-through, what does GET /api/payments/34 give as amount?

load_payment reads one row. Payment 34 is the ordinary part, ₹1,000. Only the Receipt adds the parts together.
