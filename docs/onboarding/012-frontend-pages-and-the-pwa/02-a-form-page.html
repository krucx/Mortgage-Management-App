Chapter 012 · Lesson 2

A form page: draft, Preview, confirm, save

Every screen that changes a Mortgage works the same way: load it, keep a draft, show the Preview, confirm, save, move on. This lesson reads the smallest one whole, the Change Terms page, as the pattern to copy. It opens BeforeAfter, which draws every Preview in the app. Then you build a page yourself.

By the end you can build a page from existing components, and wire a form to its Preview.

The problem

Meera's bangles (2026-Meera-PairBangles) are charged 2% a month, with a Term Change already set to 1.5% from 25 Oct 2026. The Lender now agrees to 1% from 25 Nov. Before saving, they want to see what it does: what she will owe in three, six and twelve months, and whether any figure already on her Ledger moves.

The Lender gets one try at this. A Term Change starts on an Accrual Date and changes every month after it. The screen must not offer a date the backend will refuse. It has to show the effect before the save, ask once more, and then go back to the Mortgage. Chapter 006 did this through the API. Here the Lender does it with a form, and the form must not compute any of the figures it shows.

The idea

A form page is a read page with a form inside it, in two components:

  1. The page loads what the form needs (lesson 1's pattern), then renders the form with key={id}.
  2. The form keeps the Lender's draft in state. From the draft it works out the change that saving would send (or null). It hands that to useProposedChange (chapter 011, lesson 4), which asks for the Preview, confirms and saves. It draws the Preview with PreviewSection, and after the save it goes back with navigate.

Every figure in the Preview comes from the backend, which runs the real change and rolls it back (trap #5).

The flow

Notice that the form never talks to the backend itself. It only produces change; everything that crosses the network goes through the hook and requests.ts.

The code

1. The page: load, then a form keyed by id

Lines 18–21 are lesson 1's pattern. The form is a separate component so its useState calls can start from the loaded Mortgage: useState(initial) reads initial only the first time a component is drawn. That is also why line 23 has key={id}. React keeps a component's state while its key stays the same. A new key makes React throw the old form away and draw a fresh one. Nuance (b) shows it happen.

2. The draft, and the change it makes

  1. The dates offered: the Mortgage's upcoming_accrual_dates (sent by the backend) that have no Term Change yet. The first one after the browser's today is chosen (lesson 1's two "todays").
  2. The draft starts from the Mortgage's Terms today. TermsDraft keeps the rate as the text being typed ("1.5"); fromDraft turns it into basis points (150) only when it's sent.
  3. What the draft is compared with: the Terms that will be in force just before the chosen date.
  4. Only a real change is previewed or saved. changed is false while the rate is empty or zero, and while the draft equals before.

3. Wiring it to its Preview

These five fields are all a form gives useProposedChange: change (the exact body of POST …/term-changes, or null), preview, confirm, save and saved. Summary (from ConfirmDialog.tsx, line 55) draws label/value rows as a dl.summary; every confirmation in the app uses it. useMortgageChange keeps the saved Mortgage in the cache and marks what's stale (chapter 011, lesson 5). The form's JSX then puts <form onSubmit={submit}> around TermsFields (the rate, Interest Mode and Part-Month Rule fields, shared with New Mortgage and Settings), and adds <PreviewSection preview={preview} /> and a Save button with disabled={!canSave}.

4. How a Preview is drawn

  1. A refused Preview shows the backend's sentence, the same one saving would give.
  2. refreshing dims the last answer while the next one loads (usePreview keeps it on screen, chapter 011).
  3. ChangePreview is for a dialog that has no draft, like Reopen (lesson 4): it asks once, with no wait.

One Preview answer, MortgagePreview, holds both sides of the change: before and after, each with outstanding and Projection points, plus ledger_changes. BeforeAfter draws four things from it: two headline tiles, a table of the Outstanding in 3, 6 and 12 months, the Projection chart, and the Ledger steps that change. Line 59 is the one bit of arithmetic in it, and it isn't money logic: the backend sent both figures, and this line subtracts one from the other to label the difference. If you find yourself adding interest or Principal in a component, stop and ask the backend for the figure instead.

Nuances

(a) The form opens with a change already in it

Predict: you open the bangles' Change Terms page and touch nothing. Is Save Term Change enabled? If you pressed it, what would be saved?

Save is enabled, and the Preview is already on screen. Line 40 starts the draft from the Terms in force today (2%), but line 44 compares it with the Terms in force on 25 Nov (1.5%, from the coming Term Change). They differ, so changed is true and a Preview of "2% from 25 Nov" was asked as the page opened. Pressing Save (and confirming) would add a Term Change putting the rate back up to 2% from 25 Nov. The confirmation's Before and After rows are what would catch it. On a Mortgage with no coming Term Change, the draft equals before and the page says "Change the rate, Interest Mode or Part-Month Rule to see the effect."

From scripts/screens.mjs (section L2): rate field "2", Save enabled, one Preview on opening.

(b) Keyed by id

Predict: on the bangles' form, type 1.25. Then, without reloading, go to /mortgages/2/terms (Meera's chain). React Router keeps ChangeTermsPage on screen, because it's the same route. Does the rate field still say 1.25?

No. The heading becomes "Change the Terms of 2025-Meera-GoldChainPendant" and the rate field says 2, the chain's rate. The page stayed, but its id changed, so key={id} made React draw a new ChangeTermsForm, whose useState started from the chain. Without the key, the form would keep the bangles' draft and send it as a Term Change for the chain. Every form page in src/pages/ that edits a record is keyed this way.

From scripts/screens.mjs (section L2).

(c) A Preview is the real change

Nothing here re-implements the Ledger. The Preview's "after" is the backend running add_term_change and rolling it back (chapter 006, lesson 4; trap #5). So if the Preview looks wrong, the bug is in the change on the backend, not in BeforeAfter. And a refused Preview disables Save for the reasons chapter 011, lesson 4 gives: a draft, a loaded Preview, no refusal.

Quiz

You add an "Edit Call Note" form page. Why should the page render its form as <EditForm key={id} … />?

React keeps a component's state while its type and key stay the same. Moving from one record's URL to another's keeps the same page component, so without the key the old draft would stay and could be saved against the new record. The key is React's, not the router's or the cache's.

On the Change Terms page, why does "Owed today" show no change for a Term Change from 25 Nov?

Both sides come from the backend: before.outstanding and after.outstanding. They're equal because a Term Change applies to the months that start on or after its date, and never rewrites interest already charged. BeforeAfter draws whatever it's sent.

Where does a form page's Save button get disabled from?

disabled={!canSave}. canSave needs a draft (change isn't null), no save under way, a loaded Preview, and no refusal (chapter 011, lesson 4). The form's own checks only decide whether change is null.
