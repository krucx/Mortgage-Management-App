Chapter 012 · Lesson 3

The money forms: New Payment and Closures

The two biggest forms move the most money: recording a Payment, and closing Mortgages. They follow lesson 2's pattern, with two extra jobs. The Payment form suggests how to divide a sum across Mortgages. The Closure forms close several Mortgages in one save.

By the end you can follow how a Payment is divided and how a Mortgage is closed from the screens.

The problem

Meera walks in with ₹6,600 in UPI and two Mortgages open: the chain (owing ₹83,333) and the bangles (₹1,51,500). One Payment can pay several of a Customer's Mortgages. Each Mortgage's share is an Allocation. The Lender wants a sensible split suggested, and to change it if Meera says otherwise. If she pays a Mortgage's whole Outstanding, the form must say so before saving: the Mortgage will be Redeemed and the jewellery handed back (ADR 0011).

Closing is the other big job. Kavita's anklet owes nothing but is still Active (Dormant): the Lender has to Redeem it. Arjun has defaulted on two Mortgages, and the Lender might Forfeit both at once, each with its own Recovered Amount. Every figure these forms show (each Outstanding, each profit or loss, each amount let off) must be the backend's, and closing two Mortgages must close both or neither.

The idea

The suggestion is the screen's, the rules are the backend's. allocateEqually in allocate.ts proposes equal whole-rupee shares, each at most that Mortgage's Outstanding. It's only a starting point; the Preview says whether the Payment is acceptable, as saving would.

A Redemption is read from the Preview, not worked out. If the Preview says a Mortgage would be Redeemed, the confirmation changes to "Redeem and hand back the jewellery?".

A Discount has its own confirmations. On the Payment form it is entered per Mortgage, separately from cash. Settings controls the extra-confirmation threshold, ₹500 by default; a Discount greater than that amount prompts for confirmation, and clearing the setting disables only this extra confirmation. If a Discount exceeds that Mortgage's remaining Outstanding after the cash Allocation, the form shows the reduction to the cap and asks before saving the capped amount. The Preview still decides whether cash plus Discount Redeems the Mortgage.

A Closure is a draft too. useClosing wraps useProposedChange for the three kinds (Redeem, Waive off, Forfeit), each of which takes a list of Mortgages. Each Mortgage's Closure figures are read from the Preview, and one save closes them all.

The flow

Notice where "Redeems it" comes from: the Preview's after.closure, not a comparison the screen makes. The screen can't know whether ₹83,333 pays the whole Outstanding to the paisa on a back-dated day; the backend can.

The code

1. Equal shares, each up to its Outstanding

  1. More than everything owed can't be divided within the caps.
  2. All amounts are paise; RUPEE is 100. Each share is rounded down to a whole rupee.
  3. What rounding left over goes, a rupee at a time, to the first Mortgages that have room.
  4. A Mortgage owing less than the share gets exactly its Outstanding, and the loop shares the rest among the others. for (;;) is a loop with no condition: it runs until a return.

?? uses the right-hand side only when the left is null. So a total bigger than everything owed is still split equally, with no caps, and the Preview refuses it with the backend's reason. The screen never refuses a Payment on its own.

2. The form: the date, the list, the full Outstanding

  1. Lesson 1's two "todays": on the served book, set the date back to 19 Oct 2026 first.
  2. The Outstanding of each Mortgage on the Payment's date, which may be in the past: as_of=2026-10-19. These are the caps allocateEqually uses.

"Pay the full Outstanding (₹83,333)" (line 278) fills each ticked Mortgage with its whole Outstanding, and switches to By amount. It's disabled while a new date's rows are loading, and replaced by "Can't pay it in full" (with the reason) when the backend says a Mortgage can't be Redeemed on that date.

flatMap maps each item to a list and joins the lists, so returning [] drops an item: line 203 keeps only the Mortgages the Preview has Redeemed. ...(cond && {…}) spreads the object's fields in only when cond is true (spreading false adds nothing). Paying exactly the Outstanding is a Redemption in the same request (trap #3, owned by chapter 005, lesson 3); this is the screen's half, asking the Lender before the jewellery goes back.

3. Closing several at once

  1. requestsFor (line 20) picks the Preview and save routes for the kind: POST /api/closures/redeem-preview and /redeem, and the same for waive and forfeit. Each takes a list of Mortgages, and the backend closes all of them or, if it refuses any, none (chapter 006, lesson 2).
  2. The save returns each closed Mortgage; they go straight into the cache.
  3. closureOf(id): that Mortgage's Closure as the Preview has it: its Outstanding, Final Payment, amount waived, Recovered Amount, profit or loss. The forms show these and compute none.

Two forms use it. CloseMortgage (CloseMortgage.tsx, line 40) closes one Mortgage, with three buttons: Redeem…, Waive off…, Forfeit…. It sits on the Mortgage page, and on RedeemPage at /mortgages/:id/redeem. The Closures page's ForfeitChosen (line 214) and WaiveChosen (line 343) close several at once.

Line 261 adds up the profits and losses the Preview gave, for the "Total loss" label. That's the only sum here, of figures the backend worked out.

(c) One count, two places

The menu's red dot (lesson 1) and the Closures page's Dormant list both come from the same route, GET /api/closures/dormant: the dot reads its total, the page lists its items. The backend decides what is Dormant (ADR 0012; chapter 005, lesson 5), so the two can never disagree. On the Payment form a Dormant Mortgage can't be ticked: it owes nothing, so the form offers "owes nothing: Redeem it →" instead (owesSomething, line 28).

(d) The date, again

Every form in this lesson starts on todayIso(): the Payment form, CloseMortgage, ForfeitChosen and WaiveChosen. On the served book, each one is refused until you set 19 Oct 2026 (lesson 1). On the Lender's laptop it's the right default: most Payments and Closures are recorded the day they happen.

Quiz

Meera pays ₹1,00,001 equally across the bangles (₹1,51,500) and the chain (₹83,333). What does the form suggest?

Neither cap is below the ₹50,000 share, so both get it, and the leftover rupee goes to the first in the list: the bangles, listed first (newest first). scripts/run_throughs.sh (section L3) gives [50,001, 50,000]. The chain is capped only when the share is more than its ₹83,333.

How does the Payment form know a Payment would Redeem a Mortgage?

Line 203 reads it from the Preview. The backend's record_payment decides, comparing each Allocation with the quote on its date (trap #3); the screen only asks the Lender before the jewellery goes back. Typing 83333 by hand gives the same question as the link.

On Closures you Forfeit three Mortgages, and the backend refuses the third. What is saved?

ForfeitChosen sends one POST /api/closures/forfeit with every chosen Mortgage, and the backend's transaction closes all or rolls back. In fact the Preview would have been refused first, keeping Save disabled.
