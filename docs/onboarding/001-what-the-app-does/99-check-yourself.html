Chapter 001 · Check yourself

Check yourself: what the app does

Questions from every lesson, mixed up, then an exercise on paper. Answer before you look back: recalling is what makes it stick.

Quiz

Arjun's chain: ₹45,000 on Meera's Terms, started 19 Aug 2026. What interest is added on 19 Sep?

₹900: the rate is per month, 2% of ₹45,000. ₹75 reads the rate as yearly. ₹918 compounds, but Compounded Yearly capitalises only on the first anniversary. (Lesson 2)

The Lender lends ₹25,000 in cash, with the Cash Limit at ₹20,000. What does the app do?

It warns, and the Lender decides. The Cash Limit never refuses. Meera's ₹80,000 chain was lent in cash exactly this way. (Lesson 3)

Sunita's mangalsutra was lent ₹35,000 on 21 Sep, pro-rata. On 19 Oct, 29 days into its first month, what is its Interest to Date?

₹677: 29/30 of ₹700 is ₹676.67, rounded to the rupee. ₹700 is the full-month rule, and nothing is the "nothing" rule; Sunita's Terms are pro-rata. Her Outstanding is ₹35,677, as the story's Closures page quotes. (Lesson 2)

Where does the app keep a Mortgage's Outstanding?

Nowhere. Only facts are stored (Terms, Term Changes, Closures, Allocations), and every figure owed is replayed from them, so a correction shows everywhere at once. (Lesson 5, ADR 0001)

On 25 Oct the bangles' Term Change to 1.5% starts. At what rate is the interest added on 25 Oct charged?

2%. Each month is charged under the Terms in force when it starts; the month added on 25 Oct began on 25 Sep. 1.5% first applies to the month starting 25 Oct, added on 25 Nov. A Term Change never rewrites earlier interest. (Lesson 3)

A Mortgage owes ₹10,400: ₹10,000 Principal, ₹300 unpaid interest, ₹100 Interest to Date. The Customer pays ₹500. What is left?

₹9,900 Principal. The ₹500 pays the ₹300 unpaid interest first, then the ₹100 Interest to Date, then ₹100 of Principal. ₹9,500 wrongly pays Principal first; the order is fixed. (Lesson 3)

Which word should you use in code and issues for what a Mortgage owes today?

Outstanding. "Balance" and "dues" are on the glossary's Avoid list. Outstanding has one exact meaning: Principal plus unpaid interest plus Interest to Date. (Lesson 5)

A Customer had one Mortgage and paid a month's interest each month, except one month she missed. A year later, how many Months Unpaid?

One. Each Payment pays the oldest unpaid interest first, so after the missed month every Payment pays the month before it, and one month's interest stays unpaid. That's exactly Meera's missed April. (Lesson 3, Lesson 5)

Exercise, on paper

This chapter is the only one without a run in the real repo: there is nothing to change, so nothing to undo. Hands-on work with the code starts in chapter 002. Get a pen. Use Meera's chain: ₹80,000 lent on 10 Nov 2025, 2% a month, Compounded Yearly, pro-rata. Today is 19 Oct 2026.

1. From the chain's Terms, what interest is added on each 10th?

₹1,600: 2% of ₹80,000, every month. No Capitalisation yet, so the Principal is still ₹80,000.

2. What is the chain's Interest to Date on 19 Oct, for the 10 days of October so far, pro-rata out of 30?

₹533: 10/30 × ₹1,600 = ₹533.33, rounded to the rupee. The 10 days are 10 Oct to 19 Oct, both counted, because today is always charged.

3. ₹2,800 of interest is unpaid. What Outstanding would Redeem the chain on 19 Oct?

₹80,000 + ₹2,800 + ₹533 = ₹83,333: Principal plus unpaid interest plus Interest to Date. Paying exactly that would Redeem the chain in the same step.

Check all three against The Gold Chain Story, chapter 4 ("The life of a pledge"). In chapter 002 you'll serve the sample book and check them on your own screen.

4. Name the Closure, or the status, in each case:

  1. Farida pays ₹180 short, and the Lender lets her off and returns the nose ring.
  2. Imran never comes back, and the Lender sells the earrings.
  3. Kavita's anklet owes nothing, but is still in the safe.
  1. Waived: the jewellery goes back although the final Payment fell short. ₹180 is the Waived Amount.
  2. Forfeited: the Lender kept or sold the jewellery. The ₹52,000 it fetched is the Recovered Amount.
  3. Dormant, which is not a Closure: the anklet is still Active. The Lender should check, then Redeem it with no Payment, or correct the mistyped Payment date.

Recap

  • A Mortgage is one loan against one packet of jewellery, with its own Principal and Terms, copied from the Default Terms when it is created. (Lesson 1)
  • Each Accrual Date adds a month's interest: the monthly rate times the Principal at the month's start. The Interest Mode decides when unpaid interest is capitalised. (Lesson 2)
  • Outstanding = Principal + unpaid interest + Interest to Date, exactly what would Redeem the Mortgage that day. (Lesson 2)
  • A Payment is split into Allocations, each paying unpaid interest, then the Interest to Date, then Principal. Mistakes are Voided, never edited or deleted. (Lesson 3)
  • A Mortgage ends Redeemed, Waived or Forfeited. Dormant is an Active Mortgage owing nothing, flagged for the Lender. (Lesson 4)
  • Nothing owed is stored: every figure is replayed from the facts, so changing an old fact moves today's figures at once. (Lesson 5)
  • Use the glossary's words, never its Avoid words. (Lesson 5)
