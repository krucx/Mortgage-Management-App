Chapter 001 · Lesson 4

How a Mortgage ends

October 2026 is a busy month in the sample book. Two pledges are paid off, one Customer comes in ₹180 short, one never comes back, and one anklet owes nothing though it is still in the safe. What does each ending mean, and what does the book record?

By the end you can name each way a Mortgage ends, say when each one applies, and tell a Dormant Mortgage from a closed one.

The problem

Every pledge must end, and how it ends decides two things: whether the jewellery goes back to the Customer, and how much money the Lender actually got. A Lender who just crosses out a line in the book can't say later whether the Customer paid everything, was let off a little, or lost the jewellery. Each ending has to be recorded with its date and its money, and once it's recorded, interest has to stop.

The idea

A Mortgage is Active until it has a Closure. There are exactly three Closures. After any of them, no interest accrues and no Payments are taken.

ClosureThe jewelleryThe money
Redeemedgoes back to the Customerthe whole Outstanding was paid
Waivedgoes back to the Customerthe Customer paid less; the Lender let off the rest
Forfeitedkept or sold by the Lenderwhatever the jewellery fetched

The flow

Notice that Dormant sits inside Active. A Dormant Mortgage hasn't ended. It's a warning that the book probably has a slip in it.

Run-through: October's endings

Each Outstanding below is worked out by hand from the Terms (2% a month, pro-rata, for all four), exactly as in lesson 2.

Kavita's coin: Redeemed

Redeemed means fully repaid, with the jewellery returned. Kavita borrowed ₹50,000 against a gold coin on 18 Feb 2026: ₹1,000 interest on each 18th. She paid March, April and May, then stopped. She came back on 5 Oct to take the coin home.

Check it: The Gold Chain Story, chapter 8 ("How pledges end"): "Redeemed on 5 October, paying its Outstanding of ₹54,600".

Lakshmi's necklace: a Payment that Redeems

Lakshmi paid her temple necklace's ₹4,000 interest every month. On 16 Oct she paid its whole Outstanding as an ordinary Payment. Work it out: ₹2,00,000 Principal, nothing unpaid, and 27 days of Interest to Date since 20 Sep. Chapter 002 has you check your answer on the served book.

Paying the whole Outstanding Redeems. A Payment of a Mortgage's whole Outstanding is never just a Payment: it Redeems the Mortgage there and then, so the last Payment and the jewellery's return are one step. That Allocation becomes the Mortgage's Final Payment. Chapter 005 shows how the app does it.

Farida's nose ring: Waived

Waived means closed with the jewellery returned, although the Customer's final Payment fell short of the Outstanding. The Lender lets off the rest, at their discretion. Farida borrowed ₹30,000 on 12 May (₹600 a month) and paid every month. On 16 Oct she came in to settle.

Check it: The Gold Chain Story, chapter 8: "₹30,520 paid of ₹30,700, ₹180 let off".

Imran's earrings: Forfeited

Forfeited means the Customer defaulted, and the Lender kept or sold the jewellery. Imran borrowed ₹60,000 on 10 Jun (₹1,200 a month), paid ₹2,000 once in August, and never came back. On 10 Oct the Lender sold the earrings to a jeweller for ₹52,000. That is the Recovered Amount.

Nuances

Dormant: Kavita's anklet

On 18 Oct the Lender copied Kavita's anklet into the app from the paper book: lent ₹15,000 in cash on 1 Jul, with three Payments. One of them, ₹15,000, was meant to be dated 25 Sep but was typed as 25 Aug.

Predict: with those Payments, the anklet owes nothing on 19 Oct, but Kavita hasn't had it back. Is it Redeemed?

No. It is still Active, and the app calls it Dormant: an Active Mortgage that owes nothing, so its jewellery should already have gone back. It is almost always a recording slip, here the mistyped date.

The app doesn't Redeem it automatically, because only the Lender knows whether the anklet really went back (ADR 0012). Instead it gets no Notices, stays off the Call List, and waits on the Closures page under a red dot. The Lender either Redeems it with no Payment, once they've checked, or corrects the Payment, and then it owes again and stops being Dormant by itself.

Dormant is also never stored: the app works it out from the Payments every time, so it appears and disappears as they change. Lesson 5 shows why everything works this way.

Waiving the whole Outstanding is refused

Predict: Farida had brought the full ₹30,700. Could the Lender still record it as Waived, with nothing let off?

No: the app refuses it. Paying the whole Outstanding is a Redemption, so it has to be recorded as one. A Waiver always lets something off.

Outstanding is exactly what Redeems

Every Closure above used the same figure, the Outstanding on the Closure date, Interest to Date included. There is no separate "settlement amount". That is ADR 0011's point: the figure a Customer is told is the figure that sends the jewellery home.

Reopen undoes a mistake

If a Closure was recorded by mistake, the Lender can Reopen it, with a reason. The Mortgage is Active again as if it had never been closed, and a Redemption's or Waiver's Final Payment is Voided. You'll Reopen a Mortgage yourself in chapter 005.

No closing before a Payment

A Closure can't be dated before a Payment already recorded on that Mortgage. Otherwise the book would show money paid on a Mortgage that had already ended.

Quiz

A Customer defaults, and the Lender keeps the jewellery to wear. Which Closure is it?

Forfeited: the Customer defaulted and the Lender kept or sold the jewellery. If it is kept, the Recovered Amount is what it's worth. Waived needs the jewellery to go back to the Customer. Dormant isn't a Closure at all.

An Active Mortgage owes nothing and the jewellery is still in the safe. What does the app do?

It flags it as Dormant. Only the Lender knows whether the jewellery went back, so the app never Redeems it on its own (ADR 0012). It gets no Notices, and it owes nothing, so nothing is added to what it owes.

Sunita's Mortgage owes ₹35,677 today and she pays exactly ₹35,677. What happens?

The Payment Redeems it. A Payment of the whole Outstanding is never an ordinary Allocation: the last Payment and the jewellery's return are one change. Recording it as a plain Payment would leave the Mortgage owing nothing but still Active, which is exactly the Dormant slip the app avoids.
