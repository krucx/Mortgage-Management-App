Chapter 001 · Lesson 1

A pledge, in plain words

Meera needs ₹80,000 and owns a gold chain. By the end of the afternoon she has the money and the Lender has the chain. What exactly did the two of them agree, and what does the app have to remember about it?

By the end you can say who lends what to whom, against what, and on which Terms.

The problem

In many Indian towns, when a family needs cash quickly, they don't go to a bank. They go to a local moneylender and leave a piece of gold jewellery as security. The jewellery stays in the Lender's safe. The family pays interest every month. When they repay what they owe, they get their jewellery back.

A busy Lender may hold a hundred packets of jewellery for sixty families, some for years. For each one they must remember who brought it, what is in the packet, how much was handed over and when, the rate agreed, and what has been paid since. Traditionally this lives in a paper book. Paper books get smudged, sums get added wrongly, and nobody can say quickly what a family owes today.

This app is that book, kept on the Lender's own laptop. Everything else in the codebase exists to keep this book right.

"Mortgage" doesn't mean a house loan here. In everyday English a mortgage is a loan secured on a house. In this codebase a Mortgage is one loan against one packet of pledged jewellery. The word is the Lender's own, so the code uses it.

The idea

One visit to the Lender creates one Mortgage: one packet of jewellery, one sum of money handed over, one set of rules for interest, belonging to one person. Everything the app later works out about that money starts from those few facts.

The people

  • The Lender is the one person who runs the app and lends the money. There is exactly one Lender, so the app has no logins, users or roles.
  • A Customer is someone who has borrowed by pledging jewellery. Meera Sharma is our Customer.
  • A Referrer is whoever introduced a Customer. Lenders care, because trust travels through introductions. A Referrer can be an existing Customer, or a Standalone Referrer: someone known only by name, address and phone, who has never borrowed. Meera was introduced by Ravi Verma, a Standalone Referrer.

The flow

Notice that the Mortgage, not the Customer, is the centre. Money, jewellery and interest rules all hang off the Mortgage. A Customer is just the person it belongs to.

What a Mortgage records

  • The Principal: the amount on which interest is charged. On the first day it is the sum handed over. Later lessons show it can grow and shrink.
  • How the Principal was handed over, its Lent Through: cash, UPI (India's instant phone-to-phone bank payments) or bank transfer.
  • The jewellery: a description such as "Gold chain with Lakshmi pendant", its weights in grams, and its purity in karats (24K is pure gold, 22K is the usual jewellery gold).
  • A Mortgage Name: a readable label beginning with the year it started, such as 2025-Meera-GoldChainPendant. The app suggests one; the Lender can type their own.
  • Its Terms: the rate, the Interest Mode and the Part-Month Rule. For now, read them as "the rules for how interest grows"; lesson 2 explains each part. If the starting Terms were recorded incorrectly, the Lender can correct them in Edit Mortgage; a Term Change records a later dated change.

Where do the Terms come from? The Lender sets Default Terms once in Settings, and they are copied into every new Mortgage. The Lender can change them for one Mortgage while creating it. Copying matters: when the Lender later changes the Default Terms, existing Mortgages keep the Terms they were given.

The sample book's Default Terms are not the app's. In the sample book, the Lender set the Default Terms to 2% a month, Compounded Yearly, pro-rata, and Meera's chain got those. A fresh, empty database starts with different Default Terms: 3% a month, Compounded Yearly, full month. When this course says "Meera's Terms", it means the 2% ones.

In April 2026 Meera came back with a pair of bangles and borrowed ₹1,50,000 against them. That is a second Mortgage, with its own Principal, Terms and Mortgage Name. One Customer can have many Mortgages, one per packet.

Nuances

₹1,00,000 is one lakh

The app writes money the Indian way. After the first three digits from the right, commas come every two digits: ₹1,50,000 is one hundred and fifty thousand rupees (1.5 lakh), and ₹10,00,000 is a million (10 lakh). The same figure in Western grouping would be ₹150,000. Don't "fix" this in a screen or test: it is how the Lender reads numbers.

Predict: the Terms say "2%". Meera borrowed ₹80,000. How much interest is added in one month?

₹1,600. The rate is per month, not per year: 2% of ₹80,000 is ₹1,600, every month. That's 24% a year before any compounding. Pledge lending is short and risky, so monthly rates like this are normal. If you read "2%" as yearly you would be off by a factor of twelve, so always read a rate here as per month.

What the gold is worth

The Lender keeps a Gold Rate for each purity: today's price per gram. From that the app works out each Mortgage's Collateral Value, and its Loan-to-Value: what is owed as a share of what the gold is worth. Lower is safer. On 19 Oct 2026, 20 g of 22K at ₹13,536 a gram makes the chain worth ₹2,70,720, so the ₹80,000-odd Meera owes is a comfortable share of it.

Check it: The Gold Chain Story, chapter 3 gives the ₹2,70,720, and chapter 4 its Loan-to-Value.

That's all you need for now. Chapter 008, lesson 5 covers Gold Rates and Loan-to-Value properly.

Quiz

Meera pledges her chain in November and her bangles in April. How many Mortgages does she have?

Two. A Mortgage is one packet of jewellery with its own Principal and Terms. A Customer can have many Mortgages. The bangles are never folded into the chain's Mortgage: they have their own start date and Mortgage Name.

The Lender changes the Default Terms from 2% to 2.5% a month. What happens to Meera's chain?

Nothing. Default Terms are copied into a Mortgage once, when it is created. Changing them later affects only new Mortgages. Changing an existing Mortgage's Terms is a separate, dated step (a Term Change, in lesson 3).

Ravi Verma introduced Meera but has never borrowed. What is he?

A Standalone Referrer: a Referrer who isn't a Customer. If Ravi ever borrows, he becomes a Customer and keeps the people he referred. He isn't a Customer until he has pledged something.
