Chapter 001 · Lesson 5

The Lender's day, and the one big idea

It's the morning of 19 Oct 2026. The Lender opens the app with a cup of tea. What does it show them, what do they do with it, and how can every figure on the screen be right when old Payments can still be corrected?

By the end you can describe the Lender's morning in the app, and explain why changing an old Payment moves today's figures.

The problem

The Lender has about ten Mortgages and a living to make. Each morning they need to know: what am I owed, who should I phone, who should get a reminder? And the answers must be right even though the book keeps changing backwards: a Payment entered late from the paper book, a typo Voided, a Principal corrected months later. Any one of those changes every figure after it.

The morning

Notice that every arrow out of the app ends with the Lender doing something. The app never phones, sends or closes anything on its own.

The Dashboard

The first page is the Dashboard: the whole lending book on one screen. On 19 Oct it says ₹4,02,524 is owed on 6 Active Mortgages by 4 Customers, today's Interest to Date included. It shows the book's Loan-to-Value, the Payments that came in, 2 Mortgages on today's Call List, and a red dot on Closures for Kavita's Dormant anklet.

Check it: The Gold Chain Story, Prologue ("The Lender's morning").

The Call List and Call Notes

The Call List is the day's Mortgages the Lender should phone about. A Mortgage is listed for any one of three reasons: too many Months Unpaid (3 or more in the sample book), Loan-to-Value too high (above 85%), or accruing today.

Months Unpaid counts how many of a Mortgage's latest monthly interest charges its Payments haven't covered. Capitalised interest still counts as unpaid: moving it into the Principal doesn't mean anyone paid it.

The Lender phones Arjun and records a Call Note: "Will pay after Diwali". When someone else makes the calls, the Lender prints or exports a Call Sheet, the day's Call List with a row per Mortgage to write notes on. The Excel sheet can be imported back to record all the Call Notes at once.

Notices

A Notice is a message to a Customer ahead of an Accrual Date: "on this day, this much interest will be added". Sunita's mangalsutra accrues on 21 Oct, and the sample book sends Notices two days ahead, so hers is waiting this morning. This Lender chose to approve every Notice first, so it waits for a look, then goes out on WhatsApp the same click-to-send way as a Receipt.

The one big idea: nothing owed is stored

Here is the design decision that shapes the whole codebase. Learn it now and much of the code will make sense when you read it.

The naive design, and how it fails

The obvious way to build this app is to store a running total: a row per month with "interest added" and "amount owed", updated every time something happens. The app's original spec did exactly that.

Now Kavita's anklet Payment, typed as 25 Aug, is corrected to 25 Sep. Every stored row after 25 Aug is now wrong: the interest, the unpaid interest, what's owed, whether the anklet is Dormant. Some code has to find each one and rewrite it. Miss one and the book quietly disagrees with itself, and the Lender stops trusting it.

The idea

The app stores only facts: each Mortgage's Terms, its Term Changes, its Closure, and the Allocations of its Payments that aren't Voided. Every figure owed is worked out from those facts, fresh, every time it's needed. The glossary calls this Replay. Its result, the step-by-step history of interest added, Capitalisations and Payments, is the Mortgage's Ledger.

Correct a fact, and every figure built from it is right the next time anyone looks, because nothing was ever saved that could go stale. Replaying a few hundred Mortgages takes milliseconds on a laptop, so it costs nothing. (ADR 0001)

You won't find a balance column. Newcomers often search the database for "the amount owed". There isn't one, and that's deliberate: you'll never find Outstanding, Interest to Date, Months Unpaid or Dormant in a table. Chapter 004 shows the code that replays them.

Run-through: a Preview of a change to an old fact

Because figures are replayed, the app can show what a change would do before it is saved. That's a Preview: the change is really made, then undone, so it can never disagree with saving.

History: what changed, kept for ever

Backups: the book leaves the laptop

The whole book lives on one laptop. A Backup is a copy of all of it, the database and photos, in a folder outside the app. The app writes a zip to a cloud-synced folder (Google Drive or iCloud) whenever the last Backup is a week old, and before every update that changes the database. ADR 0003 explains why not email: attachments get too big once photos pile up, and Customers' personal data shouldn't sit in a mailbox.

Quiz

A Payment from last March is Voided today. When does the chain's Outstanding change?

At once. Nothing owed is stored, so there are no figures to rewrite: the next page that asks replays the chain without the Voided Payment (ADR 0001). The original design needed a catch-up job, and that is what ADR 0001 removed.

A Mortgage has Loan-to-Value below 85% and 1 Month Unpaid, but accrues today. Is it on the Call List?

Yes. Any one of the three reasons is enough: too many Months Unpaid, Loan-to-Value too high, or accruing today. That's why Arjun's gold chain is on the list on the 19th with only 2 Months Unpaid.

Why can a Preview never disagree with what saving would do?

It makes the real change, with every check a save runs, then undoes it. So a Preview is refused exactly when saving would be. A separate copy of the rules could drift from the real ones, and a screen-side estimate could be wrong.
