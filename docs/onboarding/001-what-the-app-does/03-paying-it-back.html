Chapter 001 · Lesson 3

Paying it back

On 10 Sep 2026 Meera sends ₹12,000 by UPI for both her pledges. A month later the Lender types ₹16,000 when she paid ₹6,000. What does the app record each time, and what does the money actually pay off?

By the end you can say what one Payment does to one or more Mortgages: how it is split, what it pays first, what the Customer is sent, and how a mistake is put right.

The problem

Customers don't pay neatly. Meera owes interest on two Mortgages and hands over one sum for both. Someone else pays a little more than the month's interest, hoping to bring the Principal down. The Lender, busy at the counter, sometimes types the wrong figure. Each of these must end up in the book correctly, and the Customer needs a written acknowledgement they can trust.

A naive book would note "Meera paid ₹12,000" and leave the rest to memory. Then nobody can say later how much of it was interest, which Mortgage it was for, or whether a crossed-out line was ever real money.

The idea

A Payment is one sum from one Customer on one date. It is split into Allocations, one per Mortgage it pays. Inside each Mortgage, an Allocation pays the oldest unpaid interest first, then the Interest to Date, then Principal. The Customer gets one Receipt per Payment. A wrong Payment is never edited or deleted: it is Voided, with a reason, and the right one is recorded.

The words

  • A Payment: a single sum received from a Customer on one date, possibly covering several Mortgages.
  • An Allocation: the portion of a Payment applied to one Mortgage.
  • A Receipt: the acknowledgement of a Payment for the Customer, sent on WhatsApp or printed. A Receipt is not the Payment; it is the message about the Payment.
  • A Void: cancelling a recorded Payment with a reason, keeping it on record but excluding it from every figure.

The flow

Notice that the split is decided by the Lender (the app suggests one), but the order inside each Mortgage is fixed. Nobody chooses to pay Principal while interest is unpaid.

Why a missed month stays missed

Remember Meera's missed April. In May she paid ₹1,600, exactly a month's interest. It paid the oldest unpaid interest first: April's. May's ₹1,600 stayed unpaid. The same happened in June, July and August. Paying "this month" every month never catches up a missed one; only paying extra does. Lesson 5 shows how the app counts this, as Months Unpaid.

Nuances

Paying ahead: a Payment can reach the Interest to Date

Predict: on 10 Oct the bangles had ₹5,000 unpaid (the ₹2,000 left in September, plus ₹3,000 added on 25 Sep), and 16 days of Interest to Date had built up (25 Sep to 10 Oct, pro-rata: ₹1,600). Meera pays ₹6,000 to the bangles. What does it pay?

₹5,000 clears the unpaid interest, and the last ₹1,000 goes to the Interest to Date. That ₹1,000 is paid ahead: it counts against the month's interest when 25 Oct adds it. None reaches the Principal.

A typo is Voided, not deleted

That same 10 Oct, the Lender first typed ₹16,000 instead of ₹6,000. They Voided it with the reason "Typed ₹16,000 instead of ₹6,000", then recorded the ₹6,000 above.

The Voided Payment stays on the list, marked, so anyone can see what happened and why. Payments are never edited either: to change one, the Lender Voids it and enters the right one. A book where old lines silently change is a book nobody can audit.

One Receipt, on WhatsApp, sent by the Lender

The Receipt lists each piece of jewellery with what it was paid and what it still owes. It names each Mortgage by its description ("Gold chain with Lakshmi pendant"), never by its Mortgage Name, which is the Lender's internal label. Chapter 007, lesson 5 covers what a Receipt says.

The app doesn't send it. It opens WhatsApp with the message already typed, and the Lender presses Send. ADR 0002 explains why: driving WhatsApp automatically breaks whenever WhatsApp changes its screens, and breaks WhatsApp's terms, which could get the Lender's own phone number banned.

The Cash Limit warns, it never refuses

Government of India rules ask for large sums to go by UPI or bank transfer rather than cash. The Cash Limit is the amount (₹20,000 unless the Lender changes it) at or above which the app warns before lending or receiving that much in cash.

Predict: Meera's chain was lent ₹80,000 in cash. Did the app refuse it?

No. ₹80,000 is above the ₹20,000 Cash Limit, so the app asked first, with "Go back" as the default. The Lender confirmed, and it was saved. The Cash Limit only ever warns: the Lender decides. Her ₹1,600 cash Payments were well under the limit and asked nothing. Each Payment is judged as one sum, and so is each Mortgage's Principal.

Changing the Terms: a Term Change

Meera asked for a better rate on the bangles, and the Lender agreed to 1.5% a month from 25 Oct 2026. That is a Term Change: a dated change to some of a Mortgage's Terms, starting on an Accrual Date.

Each month is charged under the Terms in force when it starts. The month that began on 25 Sep is still charged at 2%, even though its interest is added on 25 Oct. A Term Change never rewrites interest already charged, and it can be removed until it starts.

Quiz

A Customer pays ₹5,000 to a Mortgage with ₹3,000 unpaid interest and ₹400 Interest to Date. How much reaches the Principal?

₹1,600. The order is fixed: ₹3,000 to unpaid interest, ₹400 to the Interest to Date, and the remaining ₹1,600 to Principal. The Customer can't choose to skip interest. ₹2,000 forgets the Interest to Date, which is paid before Principal.

The Lender recorded a Payment for the wrong Mortgage. What should happen?

Void it, with a reason, and record the right Payment. Payments are never edited or deleted: a Voided Payment stays on record and simply counts in no figure, so the book always shows what happened and why.

Meera pays one sum covering both her Mortgages. How many Receipts does she get?

One. A Receipt acknowledges a Payment, and this is one Payment with two Allocations. The Receipt lists each piece of jewellery by its description, with what it was paid and what it still owes.
