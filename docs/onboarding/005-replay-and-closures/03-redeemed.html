Chapter 005 · Lesson 3

Redeemed, two ways

Kavita took her coin home through the Redeem form. Lakshmi just made a Payment, and her necklace was Redeemed by it. Two routes, one result, stored the same way. This lesson follows both down to the one function they share.

By the end you can follow both ways a Mortgage gets Redeemed, and say why a Redeemed Mortgage never owes anything.

The problem

Redeemed means fully repaid, with the jewellery back with the Customer. There are two natural ways a Lender records it:

  • From the Mortgage. Kavita arrives on 5 Oct for her gold coin. The Lender opens the coin, chooses Close this Mortgage → Redeem, sees the quote, takes the money and confirms.
  • From a Payment. Lakshmi transfers the necklace's whole Outstanding on 16 Oct. The Lender records it like any other Payment, on the Payment form.

Suppose the second way recorded just a Payment. The necklace would owe ₹0 and still be Active: its jewellery should have gone back, but the book says it's held. That's a Dormant Mortgage (lesson 5), made on purpose by the app's own Payment form. And if the Payment came a rupee short, the Lender would have to notice, ask for ₹1, and Redeem by hand. ADR 0011 settles it: "paying the whole Outstanding Redeems".

The idea

Both routes end in the same function, _close_redeemed. It records the money as a Final Payment, marks the Mortgage Redeemed on that date, checks the replay and writes History. The Redeem route gets the amount from the quote. The Payment route spots an Allocation that equals the quote on its date, and hands it over instead of writing an ordinary Payment.

The combined cash Allocation and Discount can also equal the quote. The Mortgage is then Redeemed, and the Final Payment records that cash and Discount together. Reopen Voids this Final Payment, so Replay restores the Outstanding as if neither reduction had happened. Discounts on an ordinary Payment that does not Redeem are reversed by Voiding that Payment. See ADR 0014.

One tested case records ₹100,000 cash plus a ₹12,000 Discount on a Mortgage owing ₹112,000: the Payment Redeems it, but the Receipt's cash total remains ₹100,000. Reopen Voids that Final Payment and restores the ₹112,000 Outstanding. The behavior is covered by test_a_discount_can_redeem_and_reopen_voids_its_cash_and_discount in backend/tests/test_payments.py.

The flow

Notice the third arrow out of the diamond. An Allocation of the whole Outstanding is never written as an ordinary Payment. If it can't Redeem, it's refused.

The code

1. The Redeem route

  1. Not a quote this time, so a future date is refused too.
  2. The body (RedeemIn) has no amount. The app takes the quote, so the Lender can't type the wrong figure. They only say how it was paid.

2. The Payment route: _redeemed_by

  1. The same figure the Redeem form quotes, on the Payment's own date (paid_on), not today. Equal to the paisa, or it's an ordinary Payment.
  2. Lesson 2's guard. A whole Outstanding that can't Redeem raises a 422, and nothing in the request is saved.
  1. The checks every Payment gets: not in the future, the Customer's own Mortgages, all Active.
  2. Every Allocation is tested before anything is written. with _refusal_names_mortgage(…) is a context manager: if the block raises, it puts the Mortgage Name in front of the message.
  3. The ordinary Allocations, if any, become one Payment, checked by replaying (chapter 006 owns check_replays).
  4. Each Redeeming Allocation becomes a Final Payment, "received with" the ordinary part. Lakshmi's Payment had only the necklace, so there was no ordinary part, and her Final Payment is the Payment.

3. Where both meet

  1. **closure collects any extra keyword arguments into a dict. _close_redeemed passes status; lesson 4's waive also passes closure_note. They're written straight onto the mortgage row.
  2. No Payment of ₹0 is ever written. A Mortgage that owes nothing is Redeemed with no Final Payment (lesson 5's anklet).
  3. The Closure is three columns on the mortgage row: status, closed_on and redemption_payment_id. Despite its name, that last column holds a Waiver's Final Payment too.
  4. Replay the newly closed Mortgage. If the Final Payment were more than it owed, this refuses, and the transaction rolls back.

4. Why a Redeemed Mortgage never owes anything

On a Redemption, the Interest to Date is charged at most "what the Final Payment left for it": the Final Payment less everything else owed. For any Redemption made today, the Final Payment is the quote, so the cap equals the Interest to Date and changes nothing. It exists for old data. Redemptions recorded before ADR 0011 were paid under the older day count, before "today always counts". Replayed under today's count, their Interest to Date can come out higher than what was paid, so they'd owe a little forever. The cap keeps them settled, so a Redeemed Mortgage always replays to ₹0 (ADR 0011, second paragraph). You don't need to trace it, just know why it's there.

Nuances

Trap #3, owned here: paying exactly the Outstanding Redeems the Mortgage, in the same request. A Payment form with the Outstanding pre-filled is a Redeem button. A rupee less is an ordinary Payment. A whole Outstanding that can't Redeem is refused. See the traps sheet.

The three variations below were run on a scratch copy where the necklace was first Reopened, so it's Active again and owes its quote (lesson 5 shows the Reopen).

It stays Active. It owes ₹1 on 16 Oct, and ₹401 on 19 Oct. The Payment paid the 27 days of Interest to Date ahead (₹3,600), then ₹1,99,999 of Principal. But the month that began on 20 Sep is charged on its starting Principal, ₹2,00,000 (ADR 0006, chapter 004's trap #7). By 19 Oct that's 30 days, ₹4,000, of which ₹3,600 was paid ahead. Being one rupee short doesn't cost one rupee. It costs the rest of the month's interest on the whole old Principal.

(b) The whole Outstanding on a date it can't Redeem

Predict: Lakshmi's Payment is back-dated to 1 Sep, for exactly that day's quote, ₹2,01,733. Her 20 Sep Payment is already recorded. What happens?

422  2026-Lakshmi-TempleNecklace: There are Payments after that date (latest 20 Sep 2026); close it on or after then

From scripts/run_throughs.py (section L3): POST /api/payments/preview on the Reopened copy.

The amount matches the quote, so _redeemed_by treats it as a Redemption, and not_closable refuses one before 20 Sep. The app doesn't quietly save it as an ordinary Payment instead, because that would leave the necklace Dormant on 1 Sep. If the same Payment had other Allocations, they're refused with it (test_if_the_redemption_is_refused_the_ordinary_part_is_not_recorded_either). The message names the Mortgage because of _refusal_names_mortgage.

(c) A Redeemed Mortgage takes no more Payments

On the served book, a ₹1 Payment to the necklace is refused with 409: "2026-Lakshmi-TempleNecklace is redeemed: reopen it first". That comes from _refuse_unless_active in _refuse_payment. A 409 Conflict, not a 422, because the request is fine and it's the Mortgage's state that's in the way. Lesson 5 covers Reopen.

Quiz

The Redeem route's body has a method and reference. Where does the amount come from?

redeem calls outstanding_on_closure(redeem.redeemed_on).total. That's the quote for the Redemption date, which may be before today. RedeemIn has no amount field at all.

A Payment's one Allocation is exactly the quote on its date, and nothing blocks the close. What does record_payment write?

With no ordinary Allocations, first stays None and the Final Payment written by _close_with_payment becomes the Payment. That's Lakshmi's Payment 29.

Why can't a Redeemed Mortgage replayed today owe a few rupees from an old day count?

Lines 399–402 cap a Redemption's Interest to Date at what its Final Payment left for it, so it always replays to ₹0. Nothing is stored or migrated (ADR 0011).
