Chapter 005 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Questions mixed from all six lessons: work out a quote by hand, choose the Closure, predict whether cash plus Discount Redeems or is refused, and follow Reopen's reversal. Then make the quote count its own Final Payment, and see which tests notice.

Quiz

Questions 1 and 2 use Sunita's mangalsutra: ₹35,000 lent on 21 Sep 2026, on the sample book's Default Terms (2% a month, Compounded Yearly, pro-rata), with no Payments. That's ₹700 a month, and it accrues on the 21st.

1. What does closing the mangalsutra on 20 Oct take?

21 Sep to 20 Oct is 30 days, today included, so pro-rata charges the whole ₹700: ₹35,700. ₹35,677 is the quote for 19 Oct (29 days). From scripts/run_throughs.py, section 99.

2. And closing it on 21 Oct, its first Accrual Date?

On 21 Oct, ₹700 is added as unpaid, and the new month has begun: one day, ₹23, today included. 35,000 + 700 + 23 = ₹35,723 (trap #7: a month starts before the day's Payments, and today always counts).

3. On an Active necklace whose quote is ₹2,03,600 on 16 Oct, Lakshmi pays ₹2,03,599 that day. What then?

_redeemed_by only matches the quote exactly. A rupee less is an ordinary Payment, and the necklace owes ₹1 that day, then ₹401 by 19 Oct, as the month from 20 Sep is still charged on ₹2,00,000. Lesson 3.

4. A Customer owes ₹40,250 on the Closure date, pays ₹40,000, and takes the jewellery home. Which Closure is it?

Jewellery back, paid short: a Waiver. The Ledger ends with a waived step of ₹250, which is the waived_amount. Forfeited means the Lender kept or sold the jewellery.

5. Lakshmi's ₹4,000 Payments are on each 20th. A Payment of exactly the 1 Sep quote, dated 1 Sep, is recorded on an Active necklace. What happens?

It matches the quote, so it's a Redemption; not_closable refuses one before the 20 Sep Payment, and the whole Payment is refused (trap #3). Nothing is ever Voided for you.

6. Which field of a Forfeiture's Closure is read from a column, not replayed?

recovered_amount is on the mortgage row. outstanding is the quote on the Closure date, replayed, and profit_loss is recovered − outstanding, worked out in closure().

7. Kavita's anklet is Dormant. The Lender Voids its 5 Sep Payment of ₹300. What happens to the Dormant flag?

Dormant is derived on every replay. With one Allocation fewer, the anklet owes September's ₹300, so dormant() is False and the Closures list is empty. Lesson 5.

8. What does Reopening Farida's Waived nose ring do to her ₹30,520 Final Payment?

reopen clears the Closure columns, then calls void_payment on the old redemption_payment_id. The row stays, Voided, so every Ledger leaves it out. On a copy, the reopened nose ring owed ₹30,760 on 19 Oct.

9. Type Sunita's mangalsutra into the Calculator: ₹35,000 from 21 Sep 2026, the sample book's Default Terms, no planned Payments, Closed on 21 Oct 2026. What does the Outstanding tile show?

21 Oct is an Accrual Date. The month from 21 Sep (₹700) is charged that day, and the new month already has one day of Interest to Date, today included (₹23). ₹35,000 + ₹700 + ₹23 = ₹35,723: question 2's answer for the real mangalsutra, because the Simulation runs the same replay and the same quote reader. ₹35,677 is what it owes on 19 Oct, and ₹35,700 is question 1's 20 Oct figure. From scripts/run_throughs.py (sections L6 and 99).

10. POST /api/simulation is a POST. Why can't it store anything, even by mistake?

simulate(simulation: SimulationIn) has no EngineDep: it hands the inputs straight to compute_ledger. Rolling back is how a Preview works (chapter 006), on a real Mortgage. There are no Simulation tables. test_a_simulation_stores_nothing counts every table's rows before and after to pin it (lesson 6).

11. A Mortgage owing ₹112,000 gets ₹100,000 cash and a ₹12,000 Discount in one Payment. What does its Receipt report, and what does Reopen do?

The combined cash and Discount Redeem the Mortgage, but the Receipt's cash total remains ₹100,000. Reopen Voids the Redemption's Final Payment, reversing its cash and Discount. (Lesson 3, ADR 0014.)

Recap

  • The Outstanding is the last Ledger step's Principal plus unpaid interest. The Interest to Date is in it, so it's exactly what Redeems today. Every screen gets it from MortgageReplay, built by replays() in two queries, with Voided Payments left out (lesson 1, ADR 0011).
  • outstanding_on_closure(on) replays as if closed on on: other Payments that day first, then the Interest to Date is fixed, and the Final Payment comes last. closable refuses a closed Mortgage, a date before the start or before a later Payment, and a future date unless it's only a quote (lesson 2).
  • The Redeem route and a Payment of exactly the quote both end in _close_redeemed → _close_with_payment. A whole Outstanding that can't Redeem is refused, and a rupee less is ordinary. The cap keeps a Redeemed Mortgage at ₹0 (lesson 3, trap #3).
  • A Waiver is a Final Payment short of the quote, plus a waived step. A Forfeiture is no Payment plus a forfeited step. closure() reads back the Waived Amount and profit or loss; only the Recovered Amount is stored (lesson 4).
  • Dormant is Active and owing ₹0, derived and never stored, and the Lender Redeems it once they've checked. Reopen clears the Closure, then Voids its Final Payment through the ordinary Void, with the same reason (lesson 5, ADR 0012).
  • A Simulation is the inputs alone replayed by compute_ledger, closed on Closed on, and read by the same outstanding_on_closure_of as a real quote, so it owes exactly what a real Mortgage would. It touches no database. A Lump Sum larger than what is owed is refused on its row; one after Closed on is ignored. Lend this carries only the amount, start date and Terms (lesson 6).

Keep the Ledger cheat sheet open when you work on Closures: it now has the Outstanding and Closure rules. The routes sheet has every Closure route and its refusals, and the traps sheet marks #3 as this chapter's.
