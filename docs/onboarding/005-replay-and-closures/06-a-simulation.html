Chapter 005 · Lesson 6

A Simulation: a replay with nothing saved

Before lending, the Lender wants to size the loan up: "if I lend this much on these Terms, what will it come to?" The Calculator answers with a Simulation. This lesson shows that a Simulation is lesson 2's quote with no Mortgage behind it: the same engine and the same function that reads the figures off the Ledger, with nothing read from the database and nothing written to it.

By the end you can say why a Simulation owes on Closed on exactly what a real Mortgage would, follow a Calculator request through POST /api/simulation, and add an input to a Simulation.

The problem

A new Customer brings in a gold chain and asks for ₹80,000. Before she agrees, she asks the Lender: "If I pay ₹1,600 most months, what would I owe in a year to take the chain home?" The Lender has no Mortgage to look at yet: no Customer on file, no Mortgage Name, nothing in the book. They want the answer the app would give a year from now, if she did borrow.

Two obvious designs fail:

  • A separate formula on the screen. "Amount × rate × months" is wrong as soon as anything real happens. It has to copy the Part-Month Rule (pro-rata, full month, or nothing), Capitalisation on each anniversary under Compounded Yearly, month-ends like 31 Jan, the order of a day's Payments (chapter 004, lesson 3), and rounding each month's interest to the rupee. And the first time someone fixes a rule in ledger.py, the copy quietly disagrees. The Lender quotes one figure, and a year later the Mortgage page shows another.
  • Make a real Mortgage, then delete it. That needs a Customer, takes an id, writes History (chapter 006), and gets into Backups. A Preview (chapter 006, lesson 4) avoids saving, but it needs a real Mortgage and a real Customer to change, so it can't help here either.

The idea

Hand the engine the inputs alone. The amount, the start date, the Terms and any planned Payments go straight into the same compute_ledger that replays every real Mortgage, closed on the chosen date. Then read the figures off that Ledger with the same outstanding_on_closure_of that lesson 2's quote uses. The route never opens a database connection, so it can't store anything.

The planned Payments come in two kinds. A Lump Sum is a one-off amount on a date. It goes into the replay as an Allocation, the way a real Payment's share does. A monthly payment is paid on every Accrual Date, never more than is owed. It reuses chapter 004, lesson 5's HypotheticalPayments, the same thing a Projection uses.

The flow

Notice the box in the middle. outstanding_on_closure_of has two callers. One is MortgageReplay.outstanding_on_closure, which brings a real Mortgage's Ledger from the database. The other is simulate, which brings a Ledger made from the Calculator's inputs.

The code

1. The inputs and their limits: SimulationIn

  1. closed_on is the Simulation's "what if it were closed that day". Every figure the Calculator shows is as of that day, exactly as a quote is.
  2. Literal[3, 6, 12, 24, 36] means only those five values are accepted. Pydantic refuses any other with a 422 ("Input should be 3, 6, 12, 24 or 36").
  3. A Pydantic model_validator(mode="after") runs once every field has been parsed, so it can compare fields with each other. A ValueError here becomes a 422 before any code runs. MAX_YEARS is 10, and the limit is ten years of Accrual Dates, not 3,650 days.
  4. Each Lump Sum becomes an Allocation, the type a real Payment's share on one Mortgage becomes (chapter 004, lesson 1). Its payment_id is its place in the list (0, 1, 2…), so a refusal can say which row was too large. Lump Sums after Closed on are left out of the replay.

2. Replaying the plan: _Plan

  1. Replay ten years ahead with every Lump Sum. If one is too large, deal with it and try again. The loop ends once a replay gets through.
  2. Overpayment is the exception chapter 004, lesson 3's pay_through raises when an Allocation is more than everything owed on its date. For a real Payment it ends in a 422 (chapter 007). Here, _skip_or_refuse decides (snippet 3).
  3. _paid_off finds the first Payment step that leaves no Principal and no unpaid interest. Its date is "Paid off" on the Calculator, or None if it's not within ten years.
  4. The real engine, called with the inputs. The same function replays Meera's real chain. closed_on is set only when plan.closed() asks for the Ledger on the day the Simulation ends.

3. A Lump Sum that's too large: _skip_or_refuse

  1. If earlier Lump Sums had already paid everything off, this one comes after the debt is gone. It's left out and marked "after_paid_off". The tuple comparison (on, payment_id) < too_large orders by date, then by place in the list.
  2. Otherwise it's refused, as a real Payment of more than the Outstanding would be. The 422's detail names the row (lump_sum), what was owed, and the date, so the screen can show the problem on that row.

4. The route: simulate

  1. Compare with lesson 2's quote route: no engine: EngineDep, no today: TodayDep. The route has no way to reach the database.
  2. plan.closed() replays again with closed_on set to plan.ends_on(): Closed on, or the day the plan pays it off if that's sooner.
  3. The line this lesson is about. It's the same function, called the same way, as MortgageReplay.outstanding_on_closure at replay.py line 212 (lesson 2).
  4. project() (chapter 004, lesson 5) gives the graph a point on each Accrual Date over the span: what would Redeem it that day.
  5. "Total interest earned" adds each month's interest and the Interest to Date. It leaves out capitalised steps, which only move interest already charged into the Principal. Counting them would count that interest twice.

effective_annual_rate (line 40) runs project() for one year on 1015 paise with no Payments, and reports how much it grew. The huge base makes the rupee rounding of each month's interest vanish. At 2% a month that's 24% Simple or Compounded Yearly, and 26.82% Compounded Monthly.

5. A short tour of the screen

Chapter 012 owns the pages, and the Calculator is built from the same parts as the others. Four things are specific to it.

  • The inputs live in the URL. Every input is a query parameter, so a refresh, a bookmark or a link shared with someone keeps them. draftFromParams (terms.ts line 58) reads Terms from rate, mode and rule. It passes each through oneOf, so a hand-edited mode=weekly falls back to Simple instead of breaking the page. draftParams (line 70) writes them back.
  • The screen works out nothing that is owed. problems() in calculator.ts (line 47) repeats SimulationIn's limits so each problem shows beside its field before anything is sent. The backend checks them again anyway. Every figure about interest or what is owed comes from POST /api/simulation through simulationQuery, asked 300 ms after the typing stops (useDebounced, as chapter 011, lesson 4 taught for Previews). The one sum the page does itself is the jewellery's value for Loan-to-Value: net weight × today's Gold Rate in collateralOf, rounded as a Mortgage's Collateral Value is.
  • A refusal lands on its row. refusalOf turns the 422's lump_sum into the row number, and the row shows "Only ₹… owed on …".
  • Lend this opens New Mortgage with the amount, start date and Terms in the URL (lendThis.ts, lendThisLink). carriedIn (line 19) reads them back on NewMortgagePage.

rows is needed because a row with no date or no amount yet isn't sent. So the third Lump Sum sent may be the fourth row on screen. The rest of the page is ordinary: SimulationResults.tsx draws the tiles and the month-by-month table, SimulationChart.tsx draws the graph on ChartFrame.tsx (the frame the Mortgage page's Projection graph uses too, chapter 012, lesson 4), PlannedPayments.tsx holds the monthly payment and Lump Sum rows, and JewelleryValue.tsx works out a Loan-to-Value from a weight and today's Gold Rate (chapter 008).

Nuances

(a) Comparing two sets of Terms

Press Compare other Terms. A second set appears, starting as a copy of the first, and the URL gains rate2=2&mode2=compounded_yearly&rule2=pro_rata. The page sends two Simulations and shows both figures in each tile, with the difference.

Predict: change the second set's Interest Mode to Simple. Same rate, same Payments, closed on 19 Oct 2026. Does the Outstanding go down?

No, both are ₹83,333. Compounded Yearly only differs from Simple on an anniversary, when unpaid interest is capitalised. The chain's first anniversary is 10 Nov, after 19 Oct. Now try Compounded Monthly:

The tiles go back to ₹0, the line under them says "A Lump Sum is more than is owed on its date: see Planned Payments.", and Lend this is disabled. ₹83,120 is ₹80,000 + ₹2,800 + 6 days of Interest to Date (₹320), what a real Payment on 15 Oct could pay at most (chapter 004, lesson 3's Overpayment).

(c) A Lump Sum after Closed on

Move that tenth row to 25 Oct and ₹10,000. It's after Closed on (19 Oct), so allocations() leaves it out, and the row says "Ignored: after Closed on". The figures go back to ₹83,333. A Lump Sum after the plan has paid everything off says "Ignored: paid off by then" instead (after_paid_off).

(e) It stores nothing

A Simulation has no Mortgage, no Customer and no History. test_a_simulation_stores_nothing (tests/test_simulation.py line 139) counts every row of every table, runs two Simulations, and counts again. After all of this lesson's steps, the run-through's scratch book still has its 10 Mortgages, 7 Customers, 33 Payments and 34 Allocations.

If you count rows yourself, the very first request of the day (any request, a Simulation included) adds three backup_last_* rows to config. That's the daily run from chapter 010, lesson 2, not the Simulation. The run-through script found it, and after a warm-up request a Simulation changes nothing.

Simulation or Preview? A Preview makes a real change to a real Mortgage, then rolls it back, so it's refused exactly when saving would be. A Simulation has no Mortgage at all. The glossary lists "simulation" among the words to avoid for a Preview, and "quote" and "estimate" among those to avoid for a Simulation.

(f) One function, so they can't drift apart

Two parametrised tests pin the promise that a Simulation owes what a real Mortgage would. test_a_simulation_owes_what_a_real_mortgage_would_on_closure (line 38) lends a real Mortgage through the API and compares its quote with a Simulation's, for every Interest Mode, Part-Month Rule, two start dates (one on the 31st) and three Closed on dates: 54 cases. test_a_planned_simulation_owes_what_a_mortgage_with_those_payments_would (line 234) does the same with Lump Sums and a monthly payment made as real Payments: 9 more.

Predict: someone breaks the shared function. In outstanding_on_closure_of, line 80, they write unpaid_interest=last.unpaid_interest, (no - charge), so the Interest to Date is counted in both unpaid_interest and interest_to_date. How many of those 63 parity cases fail?

None. All 100 tests in test_simulation.py still pass. Both sides go through the same broken function, so they still agree with each other. Exactly 3 tests fail out of 709, and each one checks fixed figures:

  • test_closing.py::test_a_outstanding_on_closure_includes_the_interest_to_date[full_month-300000]
  • test_closing.py::test_a_outstanding_on_closure_includes_the_interest_to_date[pro_rata-70000]
  • test_replay.py::test_a_outstanding_on_closure_leaves_out_interest_to_date_paid_ahead

Checked at commit 756ae3a in a throwaway git worktree.

That's the point of sharing the function: a rule changed in one place moves the Calculator and the Close this Mortgage form together. The parity tests can't catch a bug they share, so the fixed-figure tests have to.

(g) Lend this, and what it carries

Press Lend this on the chain's Simulation. You land on

/mortgages/new?amount=80000&start=2025-11-10&rate=2&mode=compounded_yearly&rule=pro_rata

at New Mortgage's first step, choosing a Customer. Only the amount, start date and Terms go across. The Lump Sums, the monthly payment and Closed on are plans, not facts about a new Mortgage. Nothing is saved until New Mortgage is saved, so stop here: don't save it.

Lend this is disabled while the start date is still to come, with "Can't lend before the start date" beside it. That date comes from your browser's clock (todayIso), not the served book's 19 Oct 2026 (chapter 012, lesson 1 has the two clocks). A fresh Calculator also starts from your browser's today, with Closed on a year later.

Customer-facing wording rules (chapter 009) don't apply here: nothing the Calculator shows is ever sent to a Customer.

Adding an input to a Simulation

Say the Lender wants to plan a one-off fee. Every new input follows the same path, so you change the same places in order:

  1. SimulationIn in simulation.py: the field, its Field(…) limit, and any cross-field rule in closes_after_it_starts.
  2. Into the replay: through allocations() or hypothetical() if it's a Payment, or a new argument to compute_ledger if it isn't. Never work it out in simulate by hand. The figures must come from the engine, or they stop matching a real Mortgage.
  3. A test in test_simulation.py. If a real Mortgage can have the same thing, extend a parity test so the real quote and the Simulation are compared.
  4. cd frontend && npm run gen:types, so SimulationIn's type picks up the field (chapter 011, lesson 1).
  5. On the page: Inputs and problems() in calculator.ts, reading and writing it in useInputs's URL, and sending it from ask in CalculatorPage.tsx.

Quiz

Why does the Calculator's Outstanding tile match the Mortgage page to the paisa?

Both Ledgers come from compute_ledger, and both are read by outstanding_on_closure_of. The screen works out none of what is owed. And a Simulation saves nothing: there's no Mortgage to read back.

A Simulation has Lump Sums of ₹1,000 on 1 Feb and ₹1,06,001 on 15 Feb, with ₹1,05,000 owed on 15 Feb and the plan not yet paid off. What happens?

An Overpayment that comes before the debt is paid off is refused, as a real Payment would be. The 422 carries lump_sum: 1, owed and on, and refusalOf puts the message on that row. It's skipped only if earlier Payments had already paid everything off. This is test_a_lump_sum_larger_than_what_is_owed_is_refused_naming_it.

Lend this opens New Mortgage. Which of the Simulation's inputs does it carry across?

lendThisLink puts amount, start, rate, mode and rule in the URL, and carriedIn reads them back. Planned Payments and Closed on aren't facts about a new Mortgage, so they stay behind.
