Chapter 005 · Lesson 1

Outstanding: what would Redeem it today

The Mortgage page, the Mortgages list, the Dashboard, the Call List and every Receipt show what a Mortgage owes. None of them works it out. They all ask one object, MortgageReplay, which loads the Mortgage's facts once and replays them. This lesson opens that object.

By the end you can say what a Mortgage owes on any date, and where every figure in the app gets it.

The problem

Lakshmi Iyer borrowed ₹2,00,000 against a temple necklace on 20 Feb 2026, on the sample book's Default Terms (2% a month, Compounded Yearly, pro-rata). That's ₹4,000 of interest on each 20th, and she paid exactly ₹4,000 every month from March to September. In mid-October she phones: "How much to take the necklace home?"

The answer has to be the same on every screen, to the rupee. It has to include the interest for the part of October already gone, or she pays and still owes a little. And it must be cheap enough to compute for a few hundred Mortgages on the Dashboard, many times a day, on a laptop.

The app has had it wrong before. Its first design kept two figures: an "Outstanding" of interest charged on Accrual Dates plus Principal, and a separate "Redemption Amount" that added the part-month on top. Customers "were told one figure and asked to pay another" (ADR 0011).

The idea

There is one figure. The Outstanding on a date is the Principal plus the unpaid interest at the end of that day's Ledger. Since the Ledger of an Active Mortgage always ends with the Interest to Date, that's exactly what would Redeem it that day.

One class computes it. MortgageReplay holds one Mortgage's stored facts and replays them on request. replays() builds one for each of many Mortgages, loading all their facts in two queries. Every screen goes through it.

The flow

Notice the two queries at the start. However many Mortgages a page shows, their facts are loaded together, before any replaying starts.

The code

1. What a screen gets: Outstanding

A BaseModel is a Pydantic model: a class with typed fields that FastAPI turns into JSON. This one is the outstanding field of GET /api/mortgages/{id}, in paise. Note that unpaid_interest already includes the Interest to Date: on the engine's running totals, the Interest to Date step is added to "unpaid" like any other interest (chapter 004, lesson 3).

2. One Mortgage's facts: MortgageReplay

  1. The stored mortgage row: start date, amount lent, Terms, and the Closure columns (status, closed_on, redemption_payment_id, recovered_amount).
  2. A cache: one Ledger per as_of date asked for. The object lives for one request, so the cache does too.
  3. The Closure columns go straight into compute_ledger. For an Active Mortgage they're all None, and you get chapter 004's open Ledger. Lessons 2–4 show what they do when set.
  4. list(...) hands back a copy, so a caller that appends to the list can't spoil the cached one.

3. The Outstanding

That's all of it: the running totals on the Ledger's last step. There's no separate formula for the Outstanding that could drift away from the Ledger.

4. Many Mortgages at once: replays()

  1. Every Allocation of every Mortgage asked for, joined to its Payment for the date.
  2. IN (…) takes all the ids at once. That's why it's two queries for ten Mortgages or five hundred, not one per Mortgage.
  3. Every Term Change, in date order. The caller already has the mortgage rows, so nothing else is read.

For one Mortgage there's a shortcut: replay(conn, mortgage_id) at line 349 reads the row (404 if there's none) and calls replays with just that row.

Nuances

(a) A closed Mortgage owes nothing, but not on earlier dates

Predict: the necklace was Redeemed on 16 Oct. What does outstanding(date(2026, 10, 15)) return for it now: ₹0, or ₹2,03,467?

₹2,03,467, as in the table. compute_ledger replays only up to min(as_of, closed_on), and the Closure itself happens on closed_on. A Ledger asked for on 15 Oct never reaches it, so you get the open Ledger as it stood that day. On 16 Oct or later, the Ledger ends with the Closure and owes nothing. That's the glossary's "nothing once closed". Lesson 2 opens the lines that do this.

(b) Two queries, however many Mortgages

Run against the sample book's 10 Mortgages, replays() sends exactly 2 SQL statements (the script counts them with an SQLAlchemy event listener). A Lender with 500 Mortgages also costs 2. The naive version, calling replay(conn, id) in a loop, costs three per Mortgage: the row, its Allocations, and its Term Changes. The tests in tests/test_replay_once.py pin this: the Dashboard and the Reminders page replay each Mortgage once, and more Reminders take no more queries. When you write a page that lists Mortgages, load them with replays() and pass the result down.

(c) A Voided Payment never reaches a Ledger

On 10 Oct the Lender typed ₹16,000 for Meera's bangles instead of ₹6,000, Voided it (Payment 27), and recorded ₹6,000 (Payment 28). The bangles' Allocations, as replays() loads them, are Payments 23 and 28 only. Payment 27 is filtered out by the query at line 292. No code anywhere downstream has to check voided_at. A Void keeps the row for History but leaves it out of every replay.

(d) Why the cache is safe

Caching a derived figure sounds like it breaks ADR 0001. It doesn't, because of how long the cache lives. A MortgageReplay is made with its facts and never reloads them, so for that object the Ledger on a date can't change ("the facts never change, so neither does it"). It's built inside one request and thrown away at the end. After a change, the code builds a new one (check_replays calls replays() again, chapter 006), so it never reads a stale cache.

Trap #4, a warning: the cache is per object, per request. Never keep a MortgageReplay across requests, or put Outstanding in a table to speed a page up. Chapter 004 owns this trap; see the traps sheet.

(e) The words

"Balance" and "Redemption Amount" are on Outstanding's Avoid list. "Balance" hides the Interest to Date. "Redemption Amount" was the old second figure, retired by ADR 0011. Old issues and commits still use it; new code, tests and issues must not.

Quiz

The Dashboard lists 300 Mortgages. How many queries does loading their facts take?

replays() uses IN (…) over every id: one query for all Allocations (Voided ones left out), one for all Term Changes. The rows were already loaded by the caller. 300 or 900 is what a loop of replay(conn, id) would cost.

Where does the Outstanding's Interest to Date come from?

An Active Mortgage's Ledger ends with an interest_to_date step, which adds to the running unpaid interest. outstanding() just reads the last step's totals. Adding the part-month on top is the retired Redemption Amount design.

Meera's Voided ₹16,000 is still in the payment table. Which code keeps it out of her Ledger?

Line 292: payment_table.c.voided_at.is_(None). The engine never sees it, and the row is never deleted: a Void keeps the record with its reason.
